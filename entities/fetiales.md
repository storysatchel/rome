# Fetiales

The *Fetiales* (Fetial Priests, forming the *Collegium Fetialium*) were an archaic college of twenty Roman priests charged with maintaining international religious law (*ius fetiale*). Functioning as sacred ambassadors, heralds, and ceremonial guarantors of the Roman state, the fetials were responsible for demanding restitution for diplomatic injuries (*clarigatio*), ratifying solemn treaties (*foedera*) under the divine witness of Jupiter Feretrius, and performing the ritual declaration of just war (*bellum iustum*) by casting a fire-hardened cornel-wood spear into enemy territory.

```
[ Diplomatic Injury Suffered by Rome ]
                  │
                  ▼
[ Fetial Delegation Sent: Pater Patratus + Verbenarius ]
  • Wears wool fillet & sacred vervain from Capitoline Arx
  • Proclaims demands at foreign border (Clarigatio)
  • Allows 30–33 Days Grace Period for Restitution
                  │
        ┌─────────┴─────────┐
        ▼                   ▼
[ Redress Granted ]   [ Demands Refused ]
• Peace Maintained    • Return to Senate: Report Just Cause
                      • Senate & Assembly Vote for War
                            │
                            ▼
               [ Ritual Declaration of War ]
               • Fetial travels to foreign border (Archaic)
               • Later: Hurls spear into symbolic plot
                 near Temple of Bellona in Rome
               • War declared: Bellum Iustum
```

## Origins and Composition

Ancient tradition attributed the creation of the fetial law to either King Numa Pompilius (Dionysius of Halicarnassus 2.72; Plutarch, *Numa* 12) or King Ancus Marcius (Livy 1.32), who allegedly derived the ritual forms from the ancient Latins of Laurentum or the Aequicoli. 

The college numbered twenty members, appointed for life from the patrician and later plebeian nobility. Unlike ordinary political ambassadors (*legati*) appointed by the Senate for ad-hoc diplomatic missions, the fetials operated strictly under religious sanctions. 

When sent on a foreign mission, the college selected two active delegates:
1. **The `Verbenarius`:** The priest who climbed to the *Arx* on the Capitoline to pluck sacred herbs (*sagmina* or *verbenae*) together with their native soil. These sacred grasses conferred divine inviolability (*sanctitas*) upon the heralds and marked them as official envoys of the Roman gods.
2. **The `Pater Patratus` ("Created Father"):** The leading ritual officer of the delegation, formally invested by the *Verbenarius* touching his head with the sacred vervain. The Pater Patratus served as the official voice of the Roman people, empowered to take solemn oaths on behalf of the state, pronounce curses, and execute the ritual gestures of peace or war.

## Ratifying Treaties (*Foedus Ferire*)

The fetials held exclusive authority over the sacred ratification of permanent international treaties (*foedera*). The phrase *foedus ferire* ("to strike a treaty") derived directly from the fetial animal sacrifice.

During a treaty ratification ceremony:
1. The Pater Patratus recited the terms of the agreement before the foreign king or magistrates, invoking Jupiter, Janus, Quirinus, and the divine celestial order to witness Roman good faith (*fides*).
2. The priest held a sacred flint knife (*silex*) retrieved from the [[entities/temple-of-jupiter-feretrius|Temple of Jupiter Feretrius]] on the Capitoline.
3. Pronouncing an imprecation, the Pater Patratus struck down a sacrificial pig with the flint stone, praying: *"If the Roman people shall break this treaty first, through public counsel or evil deceit, do thou, O Jupiter, so strike down the Roman people as I strike down this pig today, and so much the more heavily as thou art more mighty and powerful"* (Livy 1.24).

The oath bound the Roman populace under pain of divine retribution (*ira deorum*). If a Roman commander concluded an unauthorized or shameful treaty abroad—such as the infamous surrender to the Samnites at the Caudine Forks in 321 BCE—the Senate dispatched the fetials to lead the commander back to the enemy lines naked and bound, formally handing him over (*deditio*) to purge the Roman state of religious guilt and free Rome to resume the war.

## Demanding Restitution (*Clarigatio*) and Declaring Just War

Roman theology held that no war could be successful unless it was a *bellum iustum* (a "just war" sanctioned by the gods), initiated only after the enemy had committed an unredressed injury and proper ritual notification had been delivered.

The procedure followed four distinct stages:
1. **The Demand at the Border:** The Pater Patratus, his head wrapped in a woolen fillet, approached the enemy's frontier. Crossing the border, he announced to the first citizen he met, to the border gates, and to the earth and sky that he came as the consecrated herald of Rome, demanding restitution of stolen property or surrendered criminals (*rerum repetitio*).
2. **The 33-Day Grace Period:** If the foreign city did not deliver satisfaction immediately, the fetial waited thirty or thirty-three days. At the end of the deadline, he returned to the border and called the gods of heaven, earth, and the underworld to witness that the enemy nation was unjust and refused satisfaction.
3. **Senatorial Deliberation:** The fetials returned to Rome and reported to the Senate. The consuls consulted the senators: if the Senate and subsequently the *comitia centuriata* voted to take up arms, the war was religiously authorized.
4. **The Spear-Hurling Rite:** The Pater Patratus returned to the enemy border carrying an iron-headed or fire-hardened cornel-wood spear dipped in blood. In the presence of at least three adult witnesses, he proclaimed that the Roman people declared war upon the enemy, and cast the spear across the border into enemy soil (Livy 1.32.12–14).

## The Imperial Adaptation: The Temple of Bellona

As Rome’s military operations expanded beyond Italy into Greece, Asia Minor, North Africa, and Gaul during the Middle Republic, it became practically impossible for a fetial priest to travel weeks or months across the Mediterranean merely to hurl a spear across an enemy frontier before Roman legions engaged in combat.

To preserve the indispensable ritual requirement of the *bellum iustum*, Roman legal priests devised a legal fiction:
- During the war against King Pyrrhus of Epirus (280 BCE), a captured Epirote soldier was forced to buy a small plot of ground immediately adjacent to the **Temple of Bellona** in the Campus Martius (just outside the pomerium).
- This plot of land was legally declared by senatorial decree to be foreign, hostile enemy soil (*terra hostilis*).
- From that time onward, fetial spear-hurling ceremonies were performed within the Campus Martius: the Pater Patratus stood before the Temple of Bellona and hurled his blood-dipped spear over a designated boundary column (*Columna Bellica*) onto the symbolic enemy ground.

## Revival under Augustus

By the late Republic, the fetial college had become nearly obsolete, with military commanders and proconsuls routinely declaring and waging aggressive wars without fetial sanction. 

In 32 BCE, [[entities/augustus|Octavian]] famously resurrected the archaic fetial ritual to legitimize his civil war against Mark Antony. Octavian enrolled himself as a fetial priest (*fetialis*). To avoid the appearance of declaring war against a fellow Roman citizen and consul, Octavian, standing as Pater Patratus outside the Temple of Bellona, formally hurled the fetial spear against Queen Cleopatra VII of Egypt, casting the conflict as an ancestral, religious defense of Rome against foreign eastern aggression. Under the Principate, emperors retained membership in the fetial college, utilizing fetials to sanctify client-kingdom alliances and preserve the illusion of Republican religious correctness.
