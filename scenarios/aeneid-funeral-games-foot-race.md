# The Foot Race and Its Disputed Finish

**This is a legendary/literary episode**, the second event of Anchises's funeral games in Book V of the *Aeneid*, held at [[entities/drepanum]] in Sicily. "Teucrians with Sicilians mixed" compete on a track for a prize structure announced in advance by Aeneas: every entrant gets a consolation gift, with an olive-garland podium of horse, quiver-and-gold-strap, and helmet for the top three finishers.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Nisus** ([[entities/nisus]]) — leading runner; goal: win, but ultimately prioritizes handing the win to Euryalus over his own victory
- **Euryalus** ([[entities/euryalus]]) — Nisus's beloved companion; wins first place only through Nisus's intervention
- **Salius** ([[entities/salius]]) — an Acarnanian in clear second place until fouled; goal: restore his placement via protest
- **Diores** ([[entities/diores]]) — a Trojan of Priam's line, closing fast on the leaders when the pileup occurs; finishes third
- **Helymus** ([[entities/helymus]]) — Sicilian retainer of Acestes, benefits from the chaos to finish second
- **Aeneas** ([[entities/aeneas]]) — race's judge, arbitrates the dispute

## What Happened

Nisus bolts to a commanding lead, "faster than gale wind or a bolt of thunder," with Salius, Euryalus, Helymus, and Diores strung out behind him. Near the finish, Nisus slips in a pool of sacrificial blood and gore left from the games' altar offerings and goes down hard. Still thinking of Euryalus rather than himself, the fallen Nisus deliberately trips the oncoming Salius, sending him sprawling as well — clearing the way for Euryalus to cross first "amid applause and cheers." Helymus and Diores follow in second and third.

Salius immediately protests "before the front-row elders," loudly claiming the win stolen from him by a foul; the crowd's sympathy nonetheless favors the popular, handsome Euryalus, and Diores backs Euryalus's claim too (having won third only because the result stood). Aeneas rules that the official order will not change, but personally compensates Salius with a lion's hide. Nisus then needles Aeneas publicly — if losers get consolation prizes that rich, what will the man who was actually about to win by rights get? — and Aeneas, smiling at the mud-covered runner-up, gives him an exceptional prize shield.

## Outcome

Euryalus keeps first place (a horse); Helymus takes second (an Amazonian quiver and gold strap); Diores keeps third (an Argive helmet). Salius is consoled with a lion's pelt; Nisus — technically the fastest man on the track, undone only by bad luck and his own foul — receives Didymaon's ornamental shield, a prize distinct from (and arguably richer than) the official podium rewards.

## Open Questions

- Virgil frames Aeneas's resolution as even-handed, but it effectively rewards the fouler (Nisus) as generously as the fouled party (Salius), leaving the sporting fairness of the outcome genuinely ambiguous within the text itself. ^[ambiguous]
