## Concepts

- [[concepts/ager-publicus|Ager Publicus]] — Roman state-owned public land, seized from defeated enemies; legally distinct from private land but increasingly held as de facto private possessio by occupants until privatized away by 59 BC. ( #ager-publicus #public-land #roman-law)
- [[concepts/art-and-political-propaganda|Art and Political Propaganda]] — Romans used captured art, sculpture, coinage, and engraved gems as interlocking media for elite and state self-presentation and family promotion. ( #propaganda #material-culture #elite-self-presentation)
- [[concepts/consulship|Consulship]] — The Republic's supreme annual magistracy, combining absolute military command abroad with civil headship at Rome — presiding over the senate, assemblies, and law. ( #consulship #imperium #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/contio|Contio]] — The non-voting public meeting where Republican magistrates addressed the Roman People, serving as the central (and contested) site of elite-mass political communication. ( #contio #roman-oratory #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/elite-family-strategy|Elite Family Strategy]] — The practices — continuous political achievement, inherited pedigree claims, and strategic adoption — by which Roman aristocratic families sustained multi-generational standing. ( #elite-families #roman-aristocracy #adoption)
- [[concepts/fall-of-the-roman-republic|Fall of the Roman Republic]] — Three accounts — Mommsen, Marin, and Watts — converge on a gradual, multi-generational collapse from the Gracchi to Augustus, but sharply disagree on whether Caesarian monarchy was its tragedy or its cure. ( #fall-of-the-republic #political-violence #julius-caesar)
- [[concepts/hellenism-and-roman-elite-identity|Hellenism and Roman Elite Identity]] — Roman elite engagement with Greek culture in the 3rd-2nd c. BC was a coherent, confident process of self-definition, not an anxious love-hate contradiction. ( #hellenism #roman-identity #cato-the-elder)
- [[concepts/land-and-agrarian-conflict|Land and Agrarian Conflict]] — Rome's recurring struggle over public-land distribution, from early agrarian laws to the Gracchi — though sources disagree on whether elite land-grabbing or population growth was the real driver. ( #agrarian-reform #land-conflict #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/optimates-and-populares|Optimates and Populares]] — Rather than organized parties, *optimates* and *populares* names a contested rhetorical vocabulary and a durable, largely unchallenged ideology of senatorial hegemony performed at the *contio*. ( #optimates #populares #roman-political-ideology)
- [[concepts/popular-assemblies-rome|Popular Assemblies of Rome]] — Rome's voting bodies — curiate, centuriate, and tribal *comitia* plus the *concilium plebis* — elected magistrates and passed law, distinct in function and jurisdiction from the deliberative *contio*. ( #popular-assemblies #comitia #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/roman-domestic-life|Roman Domestic Life]] — An umbrella view of Roman daily routine and family life, from the agrarian rhythm of the day to the wider economy of the household. ( #domestic-life #daily-routine #household-economy)
- [[concepts/roman-economy-and-trade|Roman Economy and Trade]] — Rome's economy ran on personalized, high-uncertainty 'bazaar' exchange and tribute extraction rather than an integrated market; concrete trade-route geography maps Bang's comparative model. ( #roman-economy #trade #bazaar-model)
- [[concepts/roman-house|Roman House]] — The physical Roman house and villa — its layout and rural buildings — as both an architectural artifact and an organizing space for household and medical life. ( #domestic-architecture #pompeii #domestic-medicine)
- [[concepts/roman-household-staff|Roman Household Staff]] — Cato's and Varro's prescriptions for staffing a working Roman farm — the overseer (vilicus), housekeeper (vilica), and laborers — and their defined duties and rations. ( #vilicus #vilica #slavery)
- [[concepts/roman-household|Roman Household]] — The Roman household (familia) as a social and legal unit of free, freed, and enslaved members bound together under the authority of the pater/mater familias. ( #familia #patria-potestas #household-economy)
- [[concepts/roman-identity-and-cultural-memory|Roman Identity and Cultural Memory]] — How Romans constructed a distinctive collective identity through founding myth, selective engagement with Hellenism, family exemplars, and material propaganda. ( #roman-identity #cultural-memory #founding-myth)
- [[concepts/roman-law-and-trials|Roman Law and Trials]] — The Late Republic's judicial system of standing courts (quaestiones perpetuae) and popular/religious tribunals, catalogued across 391 known trials from 149–50 BC. ( #roman-law #roman-trials #quaestiones)
- [[concepts/roman-magistracies|Roman Magistracies]] — The Republic's expanding set of annual offices — consuls, praetors, aediles, tribunes, quaestors, censors — each with distinct powers, census-based eligibility, and (per Sandberg) distinct assembly affiliations. ( #magistracies #cursus-honorum #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/roman-military-organization-republic|Roman Military Organization (Republic)]] — The structure, recruitment, and record-keeping of the Republican army, from clan-based early warbands through the census-based manipular legion to the eventual cohort-based late-Republic army. ( #roman-army #manipular-legion #dilectus)
- [[concepts/roman-oratory-and-political-persuasion|Roman Oratory and Political Persuasion]] — Oratory was one component — alongside ancestry, wealth, patronage, military achievement, and charisma — of a Roman politician's "public profile," central at the *contio* but never a strict prerequisite for success. ( #roman-oratory #rhetoric #political-career)
- [[concepts/roman-philosophy-schools|Roman Philosophy Schools]] — Epicureanism and Stoicism offered Romans opposing accounts of cosmic order and the grounds of a fearless, virtuous life. ( #epicureanism #stoicism #roman-philosophy)
- [[concepts/roman-provincial-administration|Roman Provincial Administration]] — Rome's gradual, improvised system for governing and extracting resources from conquered territory — allied manpower levies, war finance via tributum, and a slowly centralizing quaestorial bureaucracy. ( #provincial-administration #imperialism #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/roman-religion-and-augury|Roman Religion and Augury]] — The Roman system of divination (augury, auspices) through which magistrates sought Jupiter's sanction for public acts, and its intersection with religious courts like the Vestals' trials. ( #roman-religion #augury #divination)
- [[concepts/roman-senate|Roman Senate]] — The Roman senate began as Romulus's advisory council of patres whose descendants formed the patriciate, later becoming the Republic's standing deliberative body. ( #roman-senate #roman-republic #patricians)
- [[concepts/roman-travel-and-transportation|Roman Travel and Transportation]] — Rome's road network made the empire the first Mediterranean-wide space where ordinary travel was practical — and its vehicles became a coded moral/political language in Latin literature. ( #roman-roads #travel #vehicles)
- [[concepts/roman-urban-geography|Roman Urban Geography]] — Rome's own spatial layout (Forum, hills, pomerium) plus the empire-wide periurban pattern just outside city boundaries — a flexible zone balancing elite display and economic activity. ( #rome-urban-population #forum #topography)
- [[concepts/provinces-of-the-roman-empire|Provinces of the Roman Empire]] — How provincia shifted from a task to a territory; a gazetteer of named provinces; provincial urbanism as an exportable Roman city-model; and the empire's eventual collapse in the West. ( #provincia #empire #geography)

## Entities

- [[entities/augustus|Gaius Octavius / Imperator Caesar Divi Filius Augustus]] — Octavian/Augustus is identified by both Marin and Watts as the figure whose gradual, partly concealed consolidation of power — not any single prior crisis — marks the Republic's actual end. ( #augustus #octavian #fall-of-the-republic)
- [[entities/cato-the-elder|Marcus Porcius Cato (Cato the Elder / Cato the Censor)]] — Censor (234-149 BC): Astin and Gruen read his Hellenism/anti-Hellenism 'paradox' as calculated positioning; Marin and Hudson both use him as the paradigmatic exemplar of Roman virtus. ( #cato-the-elder #hellenism #virtus)
- [[entities/cato-the-younger|Marcus Porcius Cato (Cato the Younger)]] — Great-grandson of Cato the Censor; van der Blom's case study on him foregrounds "powerful profiling" via deliberate self-presentation rather than conventional oratorical strategy. ( #cato-the-younger #oratory #optimates)
- [[entities/cicero|Marcus Tullius Cicero]] — The Republic's best-documented orator; his speeches anchor Morstein-Marx's and van der Blom/Gray/Steel's contional-oratory studies, though van der Blom argues his career was exceptional, not typical. ( #cicero #oratory #contio)
- [[entities/clan-fabius|Fabia gens (Clan Fabius)]] — Patrician gens whose Fabii Maximi branch dominated Roman politics and warfare from the legendary Cremera disaster to Fabius Cunctator's Hannibalic War command. ( #elite-families #gens-fabia #roman-military-history)
- [[entities/gaius-gracchus|Gaius Sempronius Gracchus]] — Tribune (123-122 BC), pioneer of confrontational popularis oratory whose 'champion of the people' platform fractured along divergent interests; his own anecdotes survive via Gellius. ( #gaius-gracchus #tribunate #popularis)
- [[entities/julius-caesar|Gaius Julius Caesar]] — Late Republican general-statesman whose dictatorship and assassination mark, in both cited sources, the terminal crisis of the Republic; van der Blom treats his oratory as a career "springboard." ( #julius-caesar #late-republic #oratory)
- [[entities/jupiter|Jupiter]] — Chief god of the Roman and broader Latin pantheon; Pennell cites his cult at Alba as the religious anchor of the early Latin Confederacy's thirty towns. ( #roman-religion #latin-confederacy #regal-period)
- [[entities/lucius-licinius-lucullus|Lucius Licinius Lucullus]] — Late Republican general cited by Gołyźniak as one of the figures promoted through portraiture on engraved gems during the period when gem imagery began performing family/individual propaganda functions. ( #lucullus #engraved-gems #propaganda)
- [[entities/marcus-claudius-marcellus|Marcus Claudius Marcellus]] — General whose 211 BC sack of Syracuse is cited by Gruen as the salient but not originating moment in the Roman practice of displaying captured Greek art as war trophies. ( #marcellus #syracuse #war-spoils)
- [[entities/marius|Gaius Marius]] — Seven-time consul whose second consulship (105 BC) marked constitutional norms against successive office-holding yielding to military necessity, per Greenidge. ( #marius #late-republic #jugurthine-war)
- [[entities/mark-antony|Marcus Antonius (Mark Antony)]] — Triumvir whose career van der Blom reads through the theme of "career-making amid crisis," built on reactive rather than proactive use of oratory. ( #mark-antony #triumvirate #oratory)
- [[entities/mater-familias|Mater Familias]] — The senior female figure of a Roman household, identified by Draycott as one of the household's human members with a role in domestic health knowledge and care. ( #roman-household #household-economy #domestic-life)
- [[entities/pater-familias|Pater Familias]] — The male head of a Roman household, identified by Draycott as one of the household's human members with a role in acquiring and transmitting domestic health knowledge. ( #roman-household #household-economy #domestic-life)
- [[entities/pompey|Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus]] — Dominant general-statesman of the late Republic who built his career through military reputation and patronage rather than oratory, per van der Blom's "politics behind the scenes" framing. ( #pompey #late-republic #oratory)
- [[entities/scipio-aemilianus|Publius Cornelius Scipio Aemilianus Africanus]] — Scipio Africanus the Younger, whose 129 BC death frames Cicero's De Amicitia and who is the visionary protagonist of Scipio's Dream in De Republica Book 6. ( #scipio-aemilianus #amicitia #roman-philosophy)
- [[entities/sulla|Lucius Cornelius Sulla]] — Dictator whose 88 BC march on Rome and subsequent proscriptions set the precedent for military intervention in Roman politics that the later civil wars repeated. ( #sulla #late-republic #civil-war)
- [[entities/tribunes-of-the-plebs|Tribune of the Plebs]] — Plebeian magistracy, ten strong by the late Republic, that presided over the concilium plebis and came to dominate Roman legislative practice by the late second century BC. ( #tribunate #plebeian-assembly #roman-institutions)
- [[entities/tusculum|Tusculum]] — Latin hill town southeast of Rome and birthplace of Cato the Elder, cited by Astin as the origin of a Roman citizen family of moderate wealth, not humble poverty. ( #tusculum #latin-towns #cato-the-elder)
- [[entities/vestal-virgins|Vestal Virgins]] — Rome's order of six priestesses tending Vesta's sacred fire, whose unique liminal legal status let them act as state ritual agents and, by the late Republic, as independent political actors. ( #vestal-virgins #roman-religion #priesthoods)
