# What's in a Name? The Evolving Role of the Frumentarii

## Coverage Note
Authoritative epigraphic and institutional investigation by Stuart McCunn (*The Classical Quarterly* 69.1, 2019, pp. 340–354) tracing the origins, duties, career progression, and imperial coordination of the Roman military officers known as *frumentarii*.

## Sampled Claims and Epigraphic Analysis
- **Functional Evolution from Logistics to Intelligence:** Demonstrates that the *frumentarii* were originally legionary non-commissioned officers assigned to forage and procure grain (*frumentum*) for military units. Because grain purchasing required extensive travel, regional market inspection, and communication networks, emperors from Trajan and Hadrian onward recognized their utility as courier-liaisons and internal security operatives.
- **Headquarters at the Castra Peregrina:** Reconstructs the central command structure at the *Castra Peregrina* on the Caelian Hill in Rome, where detached frumentarii from every provincial legion were stationed under the command of the *princeps peregrinorum*, acting as a liaison bureau between provincial governors and the emperor.
- **Career Pathways (*Cursus Honoris*):** Analyzes dozens of funerary and votive inscriptions proving that secondment as a *frumentarius* was a highly prestigious, competitive pathway for legionaries seeking promotion to the centurionate (*ad spem promotionis*).
- **Operational Spectrum:** Documents their varied assignments: carrying confidential imperial dispatches, accompanying tax shipments, transporting state prisoners, conducting political surveillance on provincial officials, and executing political assassinations under Commodus and the Severans.
