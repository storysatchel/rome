# The Trial of Clodius for the Bona Dea Sacrilege

In December 62 BC the young patrician Publius Clodius Pulcher dressed as a woman and slipped into the [[entities/domus-publica|house]] of Julius Caesar, then Pontifex Maximus, to attend the rites of the Bona Dea — a festival from which men were strictly barred. He was discovered, and the resulting scandal combined a charge of religious sacrilege with gossip that he had been carrying on an affair with Caesar's wife Pompeia (Caesar divorced her, famously declaring that "Caesar's wife must be above suspicion," though he later declined to testify against Clodius). Clodius was brought to trial before a jury of senators in [[entities/rome|Rome]] in 61 BC.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Publius Clodius Pulcher** ([[entities/clodius-pulcher]]) — goal: escape conviction and political ruin; resources: family connections, personal charisma, and the backing of a wealthy patron
- **Marcus Licinius Crassus** — goal: protect a useful political client; resources: enormous personal fortune, used to bribe jurors directly
- **Marcus Tullius Cicero** ([[entities/cicero]]) — goal: see Clodius convicted; resources: his testimony demolishing Clodius's alibi (that he was thirty miles from Rome at the time)
- **The jury** — goal: self-enrichment; stakes: public credibility of the courts

## What Happened

Cicero testified against Clodius, destroying his claimed alibi and earning Clodius's lasting hatred.^[inferred] Despite the clear evidence of sacrilege, Crassus — who valued Clodius as a future political tool — bribed the jurors outright, reportedly also providing some of them with prostitutes as additional inducement. Clodius was acquitted by a narrow margin.

## Outcome

Clodius walked free, but the trial's open corruption became a public scandal in its own right, discrediting the senatorial courts. Clodius had himself adopted into a plebeian family so that he could stand for the tribunate, which he won in 58 BC; he then used the office to drive Cicero into exile in retaliation for his testimony. The episode set the stage for the later street war between Clodius and [[entities/titus-annius-milo]] (see [[scenarios/the-clodius-milo-street-war-and-trial]]).

## Open Questions

- The exact sum Crassus paid, and how many jurors were bribed versus genuinely sympathetic, is not specified in either source.
