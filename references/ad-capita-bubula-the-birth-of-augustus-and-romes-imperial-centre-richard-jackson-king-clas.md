# “Ad Capita Bubula: The Birth of Augustus and Rome’s Imperial Centre”

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- *Capita Bubula* likely denotes a geometric surveying/topographic pattern rather than literal ox heads; Suetonius’ toponym locates Augustus’ birthplace near the Curiae Veteres on the Palatine; Livia converted Augustus’ birth house into a *sacrarium* after his death; Augustan reorganization into fourteen regions and 265 *vici* made him a “second founder”; the birthplace/shrine became an ideological urban centre; evidence from agrimensores is valuable but textually/dating uncertain. ^[ambiguous]
