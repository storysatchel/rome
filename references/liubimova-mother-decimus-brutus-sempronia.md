# The Mother of Decimus Brutus and the Wife of Gaius Gracchus

## Coverage Note
Major prosopographical study by Olga V. Liubimova (*Mnemosyne* 74, 2021, pp. 825–850) resolving several longstanding genealogical puzzles regarding the family of Decimus Junius Brutus Albinus (one of the principal conspirators against Julius Caesar), his mother Sempronia (portrayed in Sallust's *Bellum Catilinae*), and the lineage of Gaius Gracchus.

## Sampled Claims and Prosopographical Proofs
- **Sempronia's Biological Motherhood:** Demonstrates conclusively that Sempronia, wife of consul Decimus Junius Brutus (cos. 77 BC), was the biological mother—not stepmother—of the tyrannicide Decimus Junius Brutus Albinus.
- **Gracchan Parentage of Sempronia:** Refutes the traditional scholarly attribution connecting Sallust's Sempronia to the Sempronii Tuditani. Through careful chronological, onomastic, and matrimonial analysis, Liubimova proves that Sempronia was the daughter of the tribune Gaius Sempronius Gracchus and his wife Licinia (daughter of P. Licinius Crassus Dives Mucianus).
- **Resolution of Cornelius Nepos's Testimony:** Explains the perplexing fragment of Cornelius Nepos preserved in late antique grammarians concerning Gaius Gracchus and a Brutus: Nepos was referring to this very matrimonial alliance connecting the Gracchan house to the Junii Bruti.
- **Political Significance in Late Republican Conspiracies:** Reveals that Decimus Brutus inherited substantial Gracchan popular clientelae and radical political sympathies through his maternal line, illuminating his mother Sempronia's active involvement in the Catilinarian conspiracy (63 BC) and Decimus's complex political trajectory between Caesar and the republican cause.
