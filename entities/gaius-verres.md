# Gaius Verres

Years before his notorious governorship of Sicily, Gaius Verres served as quaestor to the consul Carbo. Cicero's later prosecution speeches (delivered in the Sicilian extortion case) accuse Verres of embezzling from the army's funds during this earlier quaestorship and of keeping two sets of account books — one for himself, one nominally kept on the consul's behalf — to conceal the theft. Cicero frames the offense as much as a violation of the sacred personal bond between a general and the quaestor assigned to him by lot as a simple theft. ^[ambiguous]
