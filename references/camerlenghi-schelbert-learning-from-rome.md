# Learning from Rome

## Coverage Note
Architectural history study by Nicola Camerlenghi and Georg Schelbert (*Journal of the Society of Architectural Historians* 77.3, 2018, pp. 256–266) examining the transformation of the city of Rome's built environment from the high imperial period through late antiquity and the medieval era, emphasizing digital cartography, GIS modeling, and adaptive architectural reuse.

## Sampled Claims and Methodological Advances
- **Digital Spatial Humanities in Roman Topography:** Explores how dynamic mapping platforms and layered GIS databases resolve long-standing dilemmas in Roman topography by visualizing the physical coexistence of pagan monumental complexes and early Christian ecclesiastical fabric.
- **Adaptive Reuse and Spoliation (*Spolia*):** Traces the structural pragmatism of 4th- and 5th-century builders who harvested architectural elements—monolithic granite columns, Corinthian capitals, entablatures, and marble revetment—from decommissioned civic basilicas, imperial baths, and pagan temples to construct major patriarchal basilicas, notably San Giovanni in Laterano and San Paolo fuori le Mura.
- **Topographical Decentralization under Constantine:** Documents how imperial patronage under Constantine redirected the spatial gravity of monumental construction from the monumental core (Forum Romanum, Campus Martius) to the suburban periphery along major consular highways, fundamentally restructuring Roman urban flow.
