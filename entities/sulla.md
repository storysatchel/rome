# Lucius Cornelius Sulla

Sulla's career spans the two citing sources' chronological boundary: Greenidge's volume ends just before his rise (104 BC), while Campbell's survey treats his march on Rome and dictatorship as the decisive escalation that made Caesar's later civil war possible.

## Biography / Role

- Campbell identifies Sulla's march on Rome in 88 BC as the first time a Roman general led an army into the city itself, and credits him with proscriptions as dictator that may have killed "as many as nine thousand people," including the (likely apocryphal) line attributed to him: "I have executed everybody I can think of. Those I have forgotten about, I will execute at some time in the future." This figure is flagged in the citing page as stated without a specific ancient source citation, and ancient tallies for the proscription deaths vary. ^[ambiguous]
- Greenidge traces the rivalry between Sulla and [[entities/marius]] back to the Jugurthine War, already visible in embryonic form: Sulla's personal success in securing Bocchus's betrayal of Jugurtha (while serving as Marius's quaestor) set up the personal rivalry that would shape the following decades of Roman politics, including Sulla's later march on Rome. ^[inferred]
- Campbell links Sulla's proscriptions forward to the Gracchan land question as part of one continuous century-long conflict running from Tiberius Gracchus's death (133 BC) through Marius, Sulla, and Caesar to Octavian's victory at Actium — framing Sulla as the escalation point between tribunician violence and outright civil war.
- Caesar's later clemency toward former enemies, including Pompey's supporters, is explicitly contrasted by Campbell with Sulla's precedent of proscription and vengeance — Caesar's pardons are read as a (failed) attempt to break the cycle Sulla had set in motion.

## Open Questions

- The precise death toll of Sulla's proscriptions is not independently verified across the citing sources; Campbell's figure of "as many as nine thousand" lacks a specific ancient citation in the sampled text.
