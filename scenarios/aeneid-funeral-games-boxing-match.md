# The Boxing Match of Dares and Entellus

**This is a legendary/literary episode**, the third contest of Anchises's funeral games in Book V of the *Aeneid*, held at [[entities/drepanum]] in Sicily. Aeneas sets a double prize: a gold- and wool-dressed bullock for the winner, a sword and helmet "as comfort for the beaten man."

## Parties & Stakes

- **Dares** ([[entities/dares]]) — young Trojan boxer who had already killed the giant champion Butes in a prior bout at Troy; goal: claim the prize unopposed
- **Entellus** ([[entities/entellus]]) — aged Sicilian boxer, former student of the divine boxer Eryx; reluctant to fight due to age, but driven by shame
- **Acestes** ([[entities/acestes]]) — Entellus's patron and backer, publicly shames him into the ring
- **Aeneas** ([[entities/aeneas]]) — judge; equalizes the gauntlet weight between the two fighters and ultimately halts the fight to save Dares's life

## What Happened

No one in the crowd dares put on the gloves against the formidable Dares, who steps forward to claim the bull unopposed. Acestes, sitting beside the veteran Entellus, needles him publicly: "what price now that in the old days you were our strongest fighting man? Will you sit here so meek?" Entellus answers that age, not fear, has slowed him, then throws into the ring the monstrous oxhide-and-lead-and-iron gauntlets once worn by the god Eryx himself — so heavy the crowd gasps, and Dares visibly blanches and considers backing out. Aeneas, for fairness, has both men fitted with equal, lighter gauntlets instead.

The fight itself pits Dares's youth and footwork against Entellus's size and experience. Dares probes from every angle without landing a telling blow; when Entellus finally commits his full weight to a right hand, Dares slips it and the overbalanced old man crashes to the ground "as ponderously as... a hollow and uprooted pine." Acestes rushes to help his contemporary up. Far from finished, Entellus returns "hotter than before," driven by shame and anger, and batters Dares backward across the ring with an unanswered flurry, "as thick and fast as hail." Aeneas stops the fight before Entellus can kill him, and Dares is carried off by his shipmates, weak-kneed and spitting out teeth and blood.

## Outcome

Entellus wins the bull; Dares takes the consolation sword and helmet. Entellus, "glorying in his courage," then kills the prize bull outright with one blow of the gauntlet to its skull, declares the kill an offering in place of Dares's life, and formally retires from boxing — laying down his gauntlets and his art for good.
