# Not Census but Deductio: Reconsidering the Paris Relief

## Coverage Note
Major revisionist archaeological and iconographic study by Dominik Maschek (*The Journal of Roman Studies* 108, 2018, pp. 27–52) re-examining the celebrated Roman historical relief in the Louvre (MA 975, traditionally designated the 'Altar of Domitius Ahenobarbus' or 'Paris Relief').

## Sampled Claims and Archaeological Reinterpretation
- **Deconstruction of the Censorial Orthodoxy:** Maschek systematically challenges the textbook consensus established since Theodor Mommsen that the relief depicts the Roman censorial *lustrum* and registration of citizens conducted by censor Cn. Domitius Ahenobarbus in 115 BC.
- **Iconography of Colonial Foundation (*Deductio*):** Demonstrates that the central sacrificial scene represents the sacred foundation ritual of a Roman civic or military colony. The elements depict the swearing of oaths, the military muster of future colonists, the plowing of the boundary furrow (*sulcus primigenius*), and the sacrificial offering of the bull, ram, and boar (*suovetaurilia*) to Mars as patron protector of the newly deducted settlement.
- **Chronological Downdating via Military Armor:** Analyzes the armor, oval shields (*scuta*), and Montefortino/Coolus-type helmets worn by the soldiers on the frieze. These stylistic and technical details do not match late 2nd-century BC legionary equipment, but correspond firmly to the early 1st century BC (c. 90–70 BC).
- **Historical Attribution:** Proposes that the monument commemorates a major Sullan veteran colonial foundation or early Caesarian municipal deduction in Italy or the provinces, completely reframing one of Roman art's most famous monuments.
