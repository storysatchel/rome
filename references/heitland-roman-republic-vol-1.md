# The Roman Republic, Vol. 1

## Coverage Note
Volume 1 of William Emerton Heitland's classic constitutional and political study (*The Roman Republic*, Cambridge University Press, 1909; 380 pp.), tracing Roman public life from archaic regal origins down to the conclusion of the Second Punic War in 201 BC.

## Sampled Claims and Constitutional Narrative
- **Constitutional Maturation through the Struggle of the Orders:** Heitland analyzes the progressive creation of Roman public law through plebeian agitation: the first secessio plebis (494 BC), the tribunate, the Decemvirate and the Twelve Tables (451–450 BC), the Valerio-Horatian laws (449 BC), and the landmark Licinian-Sextian rogations (367 BC) which opened the consulship to plebeians and established the curule aedileship and praetorship.
- **The Dual System of Assemblies:** Reconstructs the structural mechanisms of Roman popular sovereignty, contrasting the timocratic, wealth-weighted voting structure of the *Comitia Centuriata* (skewed toward the equestrian and first-class centuries) with the territorial democracy of the *Comitia Tributa* and *Concilium Plebis*.
- **The Hegemony of Italy:** Examines Rome's diplomatic and military mastery of central and southern Italy through the Latin League, the Samnite Wars, and the Pyrrhic War, detailing the strategic planting of citizen and Latin colonies (*coloniae*) and the legal statuses of *municipia* and *civitates foederatae*.
- **The Punic Wars and Overseas Imperialism:** Details the First and Second Punic Wars, analyzing how the catastrophic defeats at Trebia, Lake Trasimene, and Cannae tested the Senate's authority, culminating in Scipio Africanus's decisive victory at Zama.
