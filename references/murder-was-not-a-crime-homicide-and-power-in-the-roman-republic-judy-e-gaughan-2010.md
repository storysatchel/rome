# *Murder Was Not a Crime: Homicide and Power in the Roman Republic*

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- During most of the Republic, intentional malicious homicide (“murder”) was not a public-law crime actionable by the government (Introduction, pp. 1–2). ^[ambiguous]
- During most of the Republic, intentional malicious homicide (“murder”) was not a public-law crime actionable by the government (Introduction, pp. 1–2).   - Treatment of homicide reflects the extent and centralization of Roman political power; decentralized republican government lacked capacity/incentive to regulate ordinary murder (Introduction, pp. 1–2, 7).   - *Patria potestas* and magisterial *imperium* embedded the right to kill in Roman definitions of power (Introduction, p. 1).   - Regal tradition attributes a murder law to Numa, linking centralized royal authority and capital punishment; source quality is difficult and legendary (ch. 1, pp. 9–16).   - The evolution from monarchy through Sulla is traced through legislation, trials, executions, assassinations, proscriptions and capital jurisdiction (contents; Introduction, p. 7). ^[ambiguous]
