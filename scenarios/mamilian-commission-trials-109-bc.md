# The Mamilian Commission: Mass Treason Trials over Jugurtha (109 BC)

In 109 BC the tribune C. Mamilius Limetanus carried a law establishing a special standing commission (*quaestio extraordinaria*) to try Romans suspected of having taken bribes from King Jugurtha of Numidia or otherwise colluded with him against Roman interests. Staffed by equestrian jurors (the *Gracchani*, so called because their composition followed C. Gracchus's earlier reform giving equites control of extortion-court juries), the commission convicted most of the senior Roman commanders and politicians it tried in a single compressed wave of prosecutions (Alexander, cases #52–57).

## Parties & Stakes

- **C. Sulpicius Galba** ([[entities/gaius-sulpicius-galba-sacerdos]]), a priest (*sacerdos*) by 109 BC — convicted.
- **L. Opimius** ([[entities/lucius-opimius]]), cos. 121 (the same Opimius who had suppressed C. Gracchus) — convicted, exiled to Dyrrachium, where he died.
- **L. Calpurnius Bestia** ([[entities/lucius-calpurnius-bestia]]), cos. 111 — prosecuted by C. Memmius; probably convicted.
- **C. Porcius Cato** ([[entities/gaius-porcius-cato-cos-114]]), cos. 114 — convicted, exiled to Tarraco.
- **Sp. Postumius Albinus** ([[entities/spurius-postumius-albinus]]), cos. 110, former proconsul in Africa — convicted.
- **A. Postumius Albinus** ([[entities/aulus-postumius-albinus]]), praetor by 111?, legate 110 — probably acquitted (it is even uncertain the trial was formally held).
- **M. Aurelius Scaurus** ([[entities/marcus-aurelius-scaurus-quaesitor]]), cos. suff. 108 — one of three *quaesitores* presiding over the commission's cases.
- **C. Memmius** (tr. pl.; later pr.) — populist tribune and prosecutor in several of the commission's cases, driving the campaign against the Numidian-war establishment.

## What Happened

The *lex Mamilia* arose from public fury (stoked above all by the tribune Memmius) at Rome's humiliating early reverses against Jugurtha, including bribed or incompetent commanders who let the Numidian king escape punishment or outright colluded with him. The resulting commission tried a cluster of the era's most senior political figures — former consuls, a legate, and a priest — in rapid succession, all under a single statute and (per Sallust and Cicero) stacked with hostile equestrian jurors eager to punish the senatorial elite they blamed for the war's mismanagement. Nearly every defendant was convicted; only A. Postumius Albinus may have escaped, and even whether his case came to trial at all is disputed in the sources.

## Outcome

The commission produced a near-total sweep of convictions against the political generation that had presided over the opening, scandal-ridden phase of the Jugurthine War: Opimius and Cato died in exile, Galba and Albinus (the elder) were convicted, and Bestia probably was too. The episode marked a rare moment when the equestrian order, newly empowered as jurors, inflicted mass political punishment on the consular nobility in a single coordinated judicial campaign — a precedent later populist politicians (including Marius) benefited from politically even without direct involvement in the prosecutions.

## Open Questions

- Whether the trial of A. Postumius Albinus occurred at all, or whether his undiminished later career (if he is the same man as the cos. 99 of that name) implies acquittal rather than non-prosecution, is explicitly left open by Alexander. ^[ambiguous]
- The precise division of cases among the three *quaesitores* is not fully recoverable from the surviving citations.
