# Penates

The Penates were Roman gods associated with the household and, particularly, its stored food and means of subsistence. They were often named with the [[entities/lares|Lares]], but the terms need not designate identical gods. The origins of the Penates and the precise form of their images in household shrines are uncertain; they should not be assumed to have formed a universally fixed pair of recognizable figures.

## The household and its food supply

The association with the *penus*, or storeroom, connects the Penates with the protection of provisions and the household's continuing welfare. Cicero describes them as ancestral and household gods. The term could also be used collectively for gods worshipped in a particular house; the suggestion that a household's choice reflected personal preference is an interpretation, not a recoverable rule for every family.

Household religion encompassed more than the Penates and Lares. The combination of divinities and the placement and number of shrines varied between homes and sometimes within one home. Offerings documented for household worship included garlands, incense, grain, fruit and wine, and occasionally animal victims. In Horace's poetic portrait of the countrywoman Phidyle, modest offerings to the household gods express a relationship of protection that does not require a rich sacrifice. This literary example illustrates an ideal, not a standardized prescription.

## Penates of Lavinium

The name also belonged to a public cult beyond the individual household. Two late ancient authors, Servius and Macrobius, report that Roman consuls, praetors or dictators performed rites at Lavinium for the Penates and Vesta together. Their accounts differ over whether the rite accompanied entry into or departure from office, leaving the timing uncertain. They also discuss whether Vesta belonged among the Penates or accompanied them. The evidence therefore points to an important shared ritual without resolving either question. For the wider institutional setting see [[concepts/roman-religion-and-augury|Roman religion and augury]].
