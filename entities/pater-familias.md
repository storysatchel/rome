# Pater Familias

The *pater familias* was the senior male figure heading a Roman household. Alongside the *mater familias*, estate manager, housekeeper, slaves, and freedpersons, he belonged to a [[concepts/roman-household]] whose members contributed in different ways to health and well-being.

## Biography / Role

- The household functioned as an active site within Rome's "medical marketplace," alongside professional and folk healing. Its head could participate in acquiring care without being a medical practitioner.
- Elite household inscriptions, including those of Livia Drusilla's *familia urbana*, attest substantial internal medical staffs: *medici*, *obstetrices*, *ad valetudinarium* infirmarians, and others. Provisioning for health formed part of managing these households, but the evidence does not assign that responsibility specifically to a *pater familias* rather than to an owner or other manager. ^[inferred]
- Cicero's correspondence records his engagement with outside physicians — Alexio, Marcus Curius, and Metrodorus — on his own behalf and that of his slave Tiro, despite lacking a dedicated household physician. This offers an example of a household head personally seeking care, not a complete definition of the role's duties. ^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- The relationship between healthcare responsibilities and the legal powers of the household head, such as *patria potestas*, is not established by the evidence considered here. ^[ambiguous]
- How were healthcare responsibilities divided or shared between the *pater familias*, *mater familias*, and other household members?
