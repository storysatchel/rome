# The Postumius–Fabius Gurges Provincial Command Dispute (291 BC)

In 291 BC, Lucius Postumius Megellus served as interrex and controversially had himself elected consul in the very assembly he presided over as presiding officer — a stark conflict of interest the Senate nonetheless allowed to stand. Meanwhile proconsul Quintus Fabius Maximus Gurges, assisted by his legate-father Quintus Fabius Rullianus, continued a Senate-authorized campaign against the last Samnite holdouts.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Lucius Postumius Megellus** ([[entities/lucius-postumius-megellus]]) — goal: claim the prestigious Samnite command for himself as consul; resources: newly won consular authority, having just presided over his own election as interrex.
- **Quintus Fabius Maximus Gurges** ([[entities/quintus-fabius-maximus-gurges]]) — goal: retain his Senate-authorized proconsular command in Samnium; resources: standing senatorial authorization, his father's presence as legate.
- **Quintus Fabius Rullianus** ([[entities/quintus-fabius-rullianus]]) — goal: protect his son's command; resources: senatorial prestige, personal relationships in the Senate.
- **The Senate** — goal: uphold its own prior authorization of Gurges's command against Postumius's attempted override; resources: authority to rebuke a sitting consul.

## What Happened

According to a fragment of Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Postumius wrote directly to Fabius Gurges in [[entities/samnium|Samnium]], commanding him to evacuate the province so that Postumius, as consul, could rightfully take command there himself. ^[extracted] The Senate, upon learning of this letter, sent Postumius a formal rebuke rather than allowing the handover. ^[extracted] The episode compounds Postumius's irregular path to the consulship (self-election while serving as interrex) with a second overreach attempting to strip an already-authorized proconsul of his command.

## Outcome

The Senate's rebuke stood in Gurges's favor; he continued as proconsul in Samnium for 291 BC with his father still serving as legate, through to the final Samnite surrender in 290 BC under the subsequent consuls.

## Open Questions

- Postumius's ultimate fate and any further consequence to his career from this overreach remain unspecified here. ^[ambiguous]
- The full text and tone of the Senate's rebuke to Postumius does not survive beyond the fragmentary notice.
