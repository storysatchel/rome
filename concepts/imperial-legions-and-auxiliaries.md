# Imperial Legions and Auxilia: Structure, Strength, and Frontiers

The permanent standing army of the Roman Principate was partitioned into two distinct institutional branches: the citizen **legions** (*legiones*) and the provincial **auxiliaries** (*auxilia*). Together with the imperial praetorian cohorts and provincial fleets, this force numbered approximately 350,000 to 400,000 men defending an empire of fifty to seventy million inhabitants.

## The Legion: Composition and Paper Strength

A Roman legion was a standardized, heavy infantry formation composed exclusively of Roman citizens:
- **Numerical Strength:** On paper, an imperial legion numbered between 5,000 and 5,280 men, divided into ten cohorts.
- **Internal Organization:** 
  - Cohorts II through X each contained six centuries of 80 men (480 infantry per cohort).
  - The **First Cohort** (*cohors milliaria*) was double-strength, comprising five centuries of 160 men (800 soldiers), guarding the legionary eagle (*aquila*).
  - A small organic cavalry detachment of 120 horsemen served as messengers, scouts, and bodyguards for the senatorial legate (*legatus legionis*).
- **Recruitment and Citizenship:** While recruited primarily from Italian citizens under Augustus, by the High Empire (reigns of Trajan and Hadrian) legionary recruits were overwhelmingly drawn from provincials possessing Roman citizenship or awarded citizenship upon enlistment.

## Total Imperial Establishment

The overall number of legions under arms evolved across the Principate:
- **Augustan Establishment:** Augustus consolidated sixty-odd civil-war legions into a standing force of 28 legions. Following the loss of three legions (XVII, XVIII, XIX) under Quinctilius Varus in the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9, the establishment settled at 25 legions.
- **Expansion to the High Empire:** New legions were raised for conquest and frontier defense (notably by Claudius, Nero, the Flavians, and Trajan). Under Trajan (who created *Legio II Traiana Fortis* and *Legio XXX Ulpia Victrix* for the Dacian Wars) and Hadrian, the army reached exactly **thirty legions**, representing approximately 150,000 to 168,000 heavy infantry legionaries.
- **Severan Peak:** Under Septimius Severus (who raised three Parthian legions), the force reached 33 legions.

In practice, combat units rarely operated at full nominal strength due to detached garrison duties, illness, casualties, and recruitment lags, but paper tables anchored imperial logistics and pay rolls.

## The Auxilia: Provincial Support and Social Mobility

The citizen legions were complemented by an approximately equal or greater number of provincial auxiliary troops (*auxilia*), numbering roughly 200,000 men by the early second century:
- **Unit Types:**
  - ***Cohortes peditatae*:** Auxiliary infantry units of 500 (*quingenaria*) or 1,000 (*milliaria*) men.
  - ***Alae*:** Elite cavalry wings (typically 512 horsemen divided into 16 *turmae*, or 1,000 in milliary *alae*), providing the tactical shock and reconnaissance capability that legions lacked.
  - ***Cohortes equitatae*:** Mixed units of infantry with attached cavalry detachments (e.g. 380 foot and 120 horse), offering flexible border patrol formations.
- **Specialized Arms:** Auxiliaries supplied specialized martial traditions absent from standard legionary equipment: Cretan and Syrian archers (*sagittarii*), Balearic slingers (*funditores*), and Numidian light cavalry.
- **The Ladder of Citizenship:** Enlisted from non-citizen free provincials (*peregrini*), auxiliary soldiers served twenty-five years for lower pay than legionaries. Upon honorable discharge (*honesta missio*), recorded on personalized bronze diplomas (*diplomata militaria*), the veteran was awarded full Roman citizenship (*civitas*) and the right of legal marriage (*conubium*), making the *auxilia* the primary engine of provincial social elevation in the High Empire.
