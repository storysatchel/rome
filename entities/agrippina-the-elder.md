# Agrippina the Elder

Agrippina the Elder (14 BC–AD 33) was the daughter of Julia the Elder and Marcus Agrippa, the granddaughter of Augustus, and the step-granddaughter of Livia. Married to Germanicus, she was the mother of the future emperor Caligula as well as Nero and Drusus Caesar, Agrippina the Younger, Julia Drusilla, and Julia Livilla.

## Germanicus and the imperial family

Agrippina accompanied Germanicus on campaign in Germany. After his death, she and her children became targets of persecution associated with Sejanus and Tiberius. Her sons Nero and Drusus Caesar were later murdered, and Agrippina herself died in AD 33.

## Posthumous memory

Caligula rehabilitated Agrippina’s memory and reputation after becoming emperor. Her image remained politically significant under Claudius: a posthumous sestertius presented her as a heroic Roman matron and, implicitly, a victim of Tiberius. These later representations made her suffering part of the dynasty’s public memory.

## Remains and dynastic commemoration

Agrippina died in prison. Caligula later recovered his mother’s remains and brought them to Rome for deposit in the Mausoleum of Augustus. Her tombstone identified her through successive dynastic relationships: Agrippa’s daughter, Augustus’ granddaughter, Germanicus’s wife, and Caligula’s mother. The inscription made remembrance of an individual inseparable from the legitimacy and continuity of the imperial family.

The Claudian sestertius is dated AD 42. Issued by Agrippina’s brother-in-law, it extended that public commemoration into a new reign. Funerary inscription and coin portrait thus provide distinct material settings for her rehabilitation.
