# Praetorian Guard Auction of Empire (193 AD)

The murder of Pertinax in AD 193 was followed by the notorious bidding contest for the Praetorian Guard’s support. The sequence linked violence in the palace to a contest in which rival candidates treated payments to soldiers as the means of securing imperial power.

## The confrontation with Pertinax

Laetus began executing praetorians while claiming to act on Pertinax’s orders. The source suggests, without treating it as certain, that he was diverting attention from himself. Two hundred praetorians went to the Palatine and forced their way into the palace. Pertinax attempted negotiation rather than calling on the vigiles or imperial horse guards to defend him.

One soldier broke the momentary hesitation of the intruders and stabbed Pertinax and Eclectus. Pertinax’s body was decapitated and his head displayed on a spear.

## Rival offers at the camp

The Guard then offered its backing to competing candidates. Didius Julianus and Flavius Sulpicianus made rival promises of cash to the soldiers. Sulpicianus offered 20,000 sestertii per praetorian; Julianus countered with 25,000.

The source warns that Dio’s theatrical description of this “auction” needs caution. Even so, the episode presents the Guard’s support as a commodity over which rival claimants bid, following its violent removal of the reigning emperor.

## The limits of purchased support

Julianus’s promise did not secure durable control. He could not pay the vast sum pledged to the praetorians, while provincial armies produced rival claimants. Pescennius Niger was acclaimed in Syria and Septimius Severus advanced from his power base in Upper Pannonia. Julianus ordered defensive preparations in Rome and had the senate declare Severus a public enemy, but these measures did not solve his dependence on uncertain military loyalty.

## Severus disarms the Guard

During the transfer of power, tribunes instructed the praetorians to leave their barracks unarmed and attend Severus. His soldiers surrounded them. Severus ordered the execution of those responsible for Pertinax’s murder and dismissed the existing praetorians, stripping them of rank and equipment. He subsequently rebuilt the Guard. The bidding episode therefore ended not with a stable contract between emperor and soldiers but with the displacement of the men whose support had been offered for sale.
