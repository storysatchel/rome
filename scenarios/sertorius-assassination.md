# The Assassination of Sertorius by His Own Officer Perperna

Quintus Sertorius was a Roman general of the Marian faction who, after Sulla's victory in the civil wars of the 80s BC, established an independent power base in [[entities/hispania|Hispania]], rallying both Roman exiles and local Iberian peoples and resisting a series of Roman armies — including one commanded by Pompey — for nearly a decade. In 72 BC Sertorius was murdered by his own officer, Marcus Perperna, ending the revolt.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Quintus Sertorius** ([[entities/quintus-sertorius]]) — goal: maintain an independent Marian stronghold in Spain against Sulla's regime and its successors; resources: Roman and Italian exiles, Iberian allies, a loyal personal following.
- **Marcus Perperna** ([[entities/marcus-perperna]]) — goal: eliminate Sertorius and assume command of his forces, reportedly motivated by jealousy of Sertorius's authority and successes; resources: a faction of officers within Sertorius's own army.
- **Pompey** ([[entities/pompey]]) — the Roman commander sent against Sertorius for years without decisive success; stakes: the assassination removed Rome's most dangerous rebel general without a further pitched battle.

## What Happened

Sertorius's long resistance in [[entities/hispania|Spain]] had frustrated successive Roman commanders, and the Roman Senate under Sulla's settlement regarded him as an outlaw. According to the atlas's summary of events, Sertorius was murdered by his own officer in 72 BC — Perperna organized the killing, reportedly at a banquet, as part of a conspiracy among discontented Roman officers in Sertorius's entourage who resented his dominance and feared the war was unwinnable.^[inferred] Perperna then attempted to take over command of Sertorius's forces.

## Outcome

With Sertorius dead, his coalition of Roman exiles and Spanish allies rapidly collapsed. Pompey defeated and captured Perperna shortly afterward and had him executed, ending the Sertorian War. The episode removed one of the last holdouts of the Marian cause from the civil wars of the 80s-70s BC and cleared the way for Pompey to return to Rome and claim credit for "ending" the Spanish war.^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- The exact motives and number of conspirators around Perperna are not detailed in the source consulted here and would require Plutarch's *Life of Sertorius* for fuller narrative.
