# The Day of Eleusis: Popillius Laenas's Ultimatum to Antiochus IV

After defeating the Ptolemies in battle and believing himself unstoppable, the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes marched his army toward Egypt in 168 BC to finish off the Ptolemaic kingdom, which had allied itself with Rome. Rather than send an army, the Roman Senate sent a single elderly senator, Gaius Popillius Laenas, to intercept him.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Antiochus IV Epiphanes** ([[entities/antiochus-iv-epiphanes]]) — goal: conquer Egypt and expand Seleucid power; resources: a massive invading army of spearmen, cavalry, war elephants, and mercenaries
- **Gaius Popillius Laenas** ([[entities/gaius-popillius-laenas]]) — goal: halt the invasion without a war; resources: nothing but the authority of the Roman Senate
- **Ptolemy V of Egypt (unnamed directly as a party in this account)** — stakes: the survival of his kingdom, which had allied itself with Rome

## What Happened

As Antiochus's massive army advanced, it was halted by the unexpected sight of a lone elderly man leaning on a walking stick. When the stranger identified himself as a Roman senator acting in the name of the Senate, Antiochus grew nervous. Popillius ordered Antiochus and his army to turn back and abandon the war against the Ptolemies.^[inferred]

## Outcome

Antiochus, afraid of provoking Roman military retaliation, obeyed and turned his army around, abandoning the invasion of Egypt entirely. Humiliated, he returned home and vented his frustration on the Jews of Judea, forbidding their religious practices and desecrating the Temple in Jerusalem — actions that provoked the Maccabean revolt led by Judas Maccabeus.

## Open Questions
