# The Fabius–Marcellus Temple Dedication Dispute (208 BC)

In 208 BC, consul Marcus Claudius Marcellus was detained in [[entities/rome|Rome]] by the board of pontiffs before he could depart for his province — over a temple he had vowed more than a decade earlier at [[entities/clastidium|Clastidium]] for a cavalry victory over the Gauls, where he had personally killed the enemy chieftain and dedicated spoils to the god Honos.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Marcus Claudius Marcellus** ([[entities/marcus-claudius-marcellus]]) — goal: dedicate a single temple jointly to Honos and Virtus, fulfilling his old battlefield vow economically; resources: consular authority, a temple already standing (built decades earlier by Fabius) that he wished to simply rededicate.
- **Fabius Maximus (Cunctator)** ([[entities/clan-fabius]]) — goal: block what the pontifical college considered a violation of sacred law, and by extension check a longtime political rival; resources: membership on the board of pontiffs, decades of religious and political authority as princeps senatus.
- **The board of pontiffs** — goal: ensure correct performance of religious ritual regarding temple dedications; resources: authority to approve or block dedications.

## What Happened

Marcellus, rather than building a new temple from scratch, wanted simply to take over the existing temple to Honos — the one Fabius himself had dedicated some twenty or more years earlier — and rededicate it jointly to both Honos and Virtus. The pontiffs, with Fabius among their number, ruled this impermissible: a single inner chamber (*cella*) of a temple could not be dedicated to two different gods under the correct forms of sacred law. ^[extracted] McCall treats the episode as a vivid illustration of how religious procedure and political rivalry intertwined in Republican Rome — the pontiffs were not simply rubber-stamping Fabius's personal grudge, since genuine ritual correctness was at stake, but the ruling also conveniently blocked a maneuver by Fabius's longstanding rival. ^[inferred]

## Outcome

Marcellus was forced to build an entirely separate [[entities/temple-of-virtus|temple to Virtus]] adjoining the existing [[entities/temple-of-honos|temple to Honos]], rather than simply rededicating Fabius's structure. The dispute delayed his departure to his consular province in southern [[entities/italy|Italy]], where he was killed in an ambush later that same year.

## Open Questions

- Whether the pontifical ruling was motivated primarily by genuine theological concerns about single-cella dedications or by Fabius's personal rivalry with Marcellus is explicitly left open by McCall as a false binary — both likely operated simultaneously. ^[ambiguous]
