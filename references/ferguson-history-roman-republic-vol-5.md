# The History of the Progress and Termination of the Roman Republic, Vol. 5

## Coverage Note
Volume 5 of Adam Ferguson's landmark Scottish Enlightenment history (*The History of the Progress and Termination of the Roman Republic*, revised edition 1805/1813), examining the political, constitutional, and military dynamics of Caesar's dictatorship, the Ides of March, the Philippics, the Second Triumvirate, and the proscriptions (47–42 BC).

## Sampled Claims and Philosophical Analysis
- **Enlightenment Civic Republican Framework:** Ferguson, a foundational thinker of the Scottish Enlightenment and predecessor of modern sociology, approaches Roman history through the lens of institutional balance, martial spirit (*virtus*), and moral corruption. He argues that republican liberty was maintained not by passive obedience, but through active civic vigilance and productive political tension between the patrician senate and plebeian assemblies.
- **The Paradox of Imperial Expansion:** Demonstrates that Rome's martial success in conquering the Mediterranean world inexorably undermined the social and economic foundations of its free constitution. Influxes of immense provincial tribute, captive slaves, and luxury corrupted the austere agrarian ethos of the citizen-soldier class, concentrating vast wealth in the senatorial oligarchy and fostering a dispossessed urban mob dependent on public doles.
- **Transformation of the Legionary Army:** Analyzes how the transition from a seasonal propertied citizen-militia to long-term overseas professional armies led inexorably to military clientelism. Soldiers looked to ambitious dynasts rather than the senatorial republic for pay, plunder, and post-service land allotments, creating the coercive machinery that ultimately extinguished republican freedom.
