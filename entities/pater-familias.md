# Pater Familias

The *pater familias* was the senior male figure heading a Roman household — a role Draycott's study of domestic medicine places alongside the *mater familias*, the estate manager, the housekeeper, slaves, and freedpersons as one of the [[concepts/roman-household]]'s constituent human members, each with distinct responsibilities for the household's health and well-being.

## Biography / Role

- Draycott's third chapter treats the *pater familias* specifically in terms of healthcare roles within the household, as part of a broader argument that the [[concepts/roman-household]] functioned as an active, under-recognized site within Rome's "medical marketplace" — one existing alongside, not subordinate to, professional and folk healing sectors.
- Elite households' epigraphic record (e.g. the *familia urbana* of Livia Drusilla) shows household heads maintaining substantial internal medical staff — *medici*, *obstetrices*, *ad valetudinarium* infirmarians, and others — suggesting that provisioning for the household's health was an expected part of managing a *familia*, though Draycott's citing page does not attribute this provisioning specifically to the *pater familias* as opposed to the household's owner more broadly. ^[inferred]
- Cicero's correspondence, cited by Draycott, shows even a prominent Roman without a dedicated household physician actively engaging outside physicians (Alexio, Marcus Curius, Metrodorus) on his own and his slave Tiro's behalf — an example of a *pater familias* figure personally navigating the medical marketplace on the household's behalf. ^[inferred: Draycott's broader claim is about Roman households generally rather than the paterfamilias role per se]

## Open Questions

- The single citing source for this page treats the *pater familias* as one named category within a chapter on household healthcare roles rather than as a subject examined independently; the specific legal and social powers conventionally associated with the role (e.g. *patria potestas*) are not elaborated in the material available from this source. ^[ambiguous]
- How the *pater familias*'s healthcare responsibilities were divided from or shared with the *mater familias*'s is not spelled out in the available excerpt.
