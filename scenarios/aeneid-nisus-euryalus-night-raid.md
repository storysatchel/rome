# The Night Raid of Nisus and Euryalus

**This is a legendary/literary episode**, a self-contained military action within Book IX of the *Aeneid* ("A Night Sortie, a Day Assault"), set during the Rutulian siege of the [[entities/trojan-camp-aeneid|Trojan camp]] in [[entities/laurentum|Latium]] while Aeneas is away seeking allies.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Nisus** ([[entities/nisus]]) — Trojan gate-sentry; goal: break through enemy lines to warn/summon Aeneas; resources: darkness, local knowledge, a javelin
- **Euryalus** ([[entities/euryalus]]) — Nisus's younger companion; insists on joining despite Nisus's attempt to spare him, out of loyalty and desire for glory
- **Ascanius** ([[entities/ascanius]]) — Aeneas's son, presiding over the besieged camp's council; approves the mission and promises lavish rewards
- **Volcens** ([[entities/volcens]]) — commander of a 300-strong Rutulian cavalry patrol that intercepts the pair at dawn

## What Happened

Standing watch at night, Nisus proposes to Euryalus that the two of them slip through the besieging Rutulian lines — whose watchfires burn carelessly thin — to find Aeneas. Euryalus insists on coming along despite Nisus's reluctance to risk him; the Trojan war council, led by the boy Ascanius in his father's absence, grants permission and promises the pair rich rewards (arms, horses, land) for the mission.

Moving through the sleeping enemy camp, the pair find Rutulian soldiers scattered in drunken or exhausted sleep around dying campfires. Nisus kills the augur-king Rhamnes along with several bodyguards, Remus's armorer and charioteer, and beheads Remus himself; Euryalus matches him, killing Fadus, Herbesus, Rhoetus (who wakes and is run through), and Abaris, among unnamed "nameless ranks of sleeping soldiery." Nisus calls a halt as dawn nears — "we've made them pay enough" — but Euryalus has already looted a golden belt and medals from Rhamnes's body and donned a captured plumed helmet belonging to Messapus, ornaments that catch moonlight as the two retreat.

A 300-strong cavalry patrol under Volcens, returning to camp, spots the gleam of Euryalus's stolen helmet and surrounds the wood the pair have fled into. Euryalus, slowed and disoriented by the dark undergrowth and his plunder, is separated from Nisus and captured. Nisus doubles back, and — unseen — kills two of the captors, Sulmo and Tagus, with thrown javelins from the treeline. Enraged and unable to find the hidden attacker, Volcens turns his sword on the captive Euryalus to make him "pay" for both deaths. Nisus breaks cover, screaming that he alone is responsible and begging the Rutulians to kill him instead — but Volcens kills Euryalus anyway. Nisus then charges the entire patrol alone, kills Volcens, and is cut down and killed on top of his friend's body.

## Outcome

Both Trojans die without reaching Aeneas; the mission's stated objective fails. But the Rutulians suffer heavy losses (Rhamnes, Volcens, and many named captains among the dead) and mourn them visibly on returning to camp. Virgil explicitly intervenes as narrator to promise the pair eternal literary remembrance ("no future day will ever take you out of the record of remembering Time"), framing their deaths as a paradigm of *amicitia* (loyal friendship) rather than a military success.
