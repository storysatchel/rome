# Cicero, Roman Republicanism and the Contested Meaning of Libertas

## Coverage Note
Substantive political theory and historical analysis by Geoff Kennedy (*Political Studies* 62, 2014, pp. 488–501) critically re-evaluating Roman Republican political thought through Cicero's dialogues *De Re Publica* and *De Legibus*.

## Sampled Claims and Theoretical Critique
- **Critique of Neo-Roman Republicanism:** Directly challenges the modern neo-Roman republican theories of Quentin Skinner and Philip Pettit, which define republican liberty strictly as "non-domination." Kennedy demonstrates that these modern theorists abstract Roman political concepts from the actual material conditions of Roman class domination, slavery, and oligopoly.
- **Concordia and Proportionate Equality (*Aequitas*):** Shows that Cicero's ideal commonwealth was explicitly anti-egalitarian. For Cicero, true justice and harmony (*concordia ordinum*) required recognizing fundamental differences in birth, wealth, and virtue. True *aequitas* was geometric/proportionate equality: allocating political power in proportion to social dignity (*dignitas*), ensuring that the aristocratic Senate maintained supreme authority over the popular masses.
- **Defense of Timocratic Voting:** Analyzes Cicero's ardent defense of the timocratic voting hierarchy of the *Comitia Centuriata* (where the wealthiest citizens held an absolute voting majority) and his hostility to the secret ballot (*lex tabellaria*), which Cicero viewed as a dangerous mechanism that severed clients from the oversight and control of their aristocratic patrons.
- **Contested Libertas:** Concludes that *libertas* was an ideological battleground in late Republican Rome: popular reformers invoked *libertas* to mean democratic freedom from senatorial oppression, while the optimate elite invoked it to justify aristocratic supremacy against tyranny.
