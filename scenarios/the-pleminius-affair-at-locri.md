# The Pleminius Affair at Locri — A Garrison Atrocity Becomes a Senate Proxy War

In 205 BC Scipio Africanus occupied the southern Italian port of Locri and placed his legate Gaius Pleminius in command before returning to Sicily. Pleminius's garrison, per Livy, "so outdid the garrison commander Hamilcar" in rapacity that it seemed a contest in villainy rather than arms, including the plundering of the temple treasury of the goddess Proserpina. The affair boiled over into violence and ultimately became ammunition in a Senate struggle between Scipio's supporters and detractors, led by Fabius Maximus.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Gaius Pleminius** ([[entities/gaius-pleminius]]) — goal: retain control of Locri and punish perceived insubordination; resources: garrison command, lictors.
- **Sergius and Matienus** ([[entities/sergius-and-matienus]]) — goal: enforce discipline among their own detachment and protect a Locrian victim of theft; resources: command of an independent detachment, loyalty of their men.
- **Scipio Africanus** ([[entities/scipio-africanus]]) — goal: protect his legate, maintain control of his command, and proceed with his planned invasion of Africa; resources: proconsular authority, broad popular support, 7,000 volunteer soldiers.
- **Fabius Maximus (Cunctator)** ([[entities/clan-fabius]]) — goal: discredit Scipio and block or delay the African invasion; resources: position as princeps senatus, decades of senatorial prestige.
- **The Locrians** — goal: obtain redress for atrocities and temple desecration; resources: a ten-member embassy sent to petition the Senate directly.

## What Happened

A soldier under Pleminius's command stole a silver cup from a Locrian; fleeing the scene, he ran into Sergius and Matienus, two military tribunes commanding an independent detachment, who moved to return the cup. A brawl broke out between the men loyal to each side. Pleminius had the two tribunes seized, stripped, and prepared for a flogging; their own men intervened, overwhelmed Pleminius's lictors, and mutilated Pleminius's ears and nose in the melee. ^[extracted]

Scipio sailed from Syracuse to investigate, cleared Pleminius of wrongdoing, and sent the two tribunes to Rome in chains for the Senate to judge — but left before ensuring they actually departed. With Scipio gone, Pleminius, disfigured and resentful, turned his fury on the leading citizens of Locri who had complained to Scipio, inflicting fresh abuses. ^[extracted] A ten-member Locrian embassy reached Rome and described daily robbery, assault, rape, and the desecration of Proserpina's temple — whose plundering, the Locrians warned, had once cursed King Pyrrhus with defeat and death. ^[extracted] In the ensuing Senate debate, princeps senatus Fabius Maximus pressed the Locrians on why they had not appealed directly to Scipio (they explained Scipio already favored Pleminius) and used the episode to question Scipio's fitness for command, reviving charges that Scipio was too lax with his troops and "too Greek" in dress and manner.

## Outcome

Fabius's motion to summarily condemn or recall Scipio failed; instead, a motion by Quintus Metellus prevailed, and the Senate sent an investigative commission to Locri rather than retiring Scipio from command. The commission found the Locrians still blamed Pleminius rather than Scipio, though they also judged Scipio too lax an overseer. Scipio retained his command and proceeded toward the invasion of Africa; Fabius failed to use the scandal to halt it.

## Open Questions

- Pleminius's own ultimate legal fate after the commission's investigation is not detailed in the summarized account.
- Whether Scipio genuinely bore responsibility for Pleminius's conduct, or was merely an absentee commander exploited by his political rivals, remains explicitly contested within the ancient tradition itself. ^[ambiguous]
