# *Rome’s Imperial Economy: Twelve Essays*

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- The collection studies structural features of the Roman economy: class, poverty/destitution, slavery, production, trade, money and period overviews (contents pp. 8–9). ^[ambiguous]
- Harris argues that the Roman economy must be analyzed across the whole Empire, not Rome and Italy alone, while avoiding an unsupported claim of complete market integration (introduction PDF pp. 21–22). ^[ambiguous]
- Slavery was central and the slave trade drew on multiple sources, including warfare and abandoned infants; material evidence complicates simplistic models (pp. 17–19). ^[ambiguous]
- Romans developed complex commercial mechanisms, production/distribution systems and economically significant long-distance trade (pp. 18–20). ^[ambiguous]
- Credit-money was important and should not be equated mechanically with the volume of coinage (pp. 20–21). ^[ambiguous]
- Late-Republican growth indicators may show per-capita gains for some populations, but internal war and unequal distribution complicate claims about whole-imperial welfare (pp. 20–22). ^[ambiguous]
- Harris describes a broad downward economic trend from about AD 200 to 700, with western decline earlier than eastern decline, while acknowledging regional variation and debate (pp. 23–26). ^[ambiguous]
