# Roman Senate

The Roman senate traces its origin, in the ancient tradition Botsford surveys, to Romulus's selection of a council of elders at the city's founding — the body whose descendants became the patriciate and who themselves became the original senate.

## Key Ideas

- Ancient authors (Cicero, Livy, Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Plutarch) uniformly derive the patriciate from Romulus's selection of a council of elders (the *patres*), who became the original senate, with *patricii* denoting their descendants. Botsford shows the competing etymological tradition deriving *patricius* from *patrem ciere* ("one who can cite a father") created lasting confusion by conflating the term with *ingenuus* (freeborn), muddying the senate's own origin story along with the patriciate's.
- Because this single claim is the only material Botsford's monograph on the assemblies devotes specifically to the senate (his book's focus being the *comitia*, not the senate itself), the senate's institutional machinery in the middle and late Republic — composition, the *lectio senatus*, debate procedure, decrees (*senatus consulta*) — is not covered by this source and remains outside this page's evidentiary base. ^[inferred]
- The senate's formal relationship to the voting assemblies — confirming magistrates' *imperium*, granting the *patrum auctoritas* that validated comitial decisions, and receiving reports in the *contio* — is treated at greater length in sources behind [[concepts/popular-assemblies-rome]], [[concepts/consulship]], and [[concepts/roman-magistracies]] rather than here, to avoid duplicating claims this page's own citing source does not make. ^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- Whether the patriciate (and by extension the earliest senate) was from the outset an exclusively patrician institution, or whether plebeians and clients also had some standing in the pre-Republican populace, is a question Botsford argues at length but does not consider fully settled.
- The precise size, composition, and procedural rules of the senate across the Republic are not addressed by the one citing source behind this page; a thicker Roman-senate page would need sources specifically about senatorial procedure (e.g. on the *lectio senatus* or *senatus consulta*) rather than assembly-focused scholarship. ^[ambiguous]
