# The Ship Race at Anchises's Funeral Games

**This is a legendary/literary episode, not a historical event** — it is narrated by Virgil in Book V of the *Aeneid* as part of the funeral games Aeneas holds at [[entities/drepanum]] in Sicily on the first anniversary of his father Anchises's death. Four warships race out to a [[entities/drepanum-turning-rock|sea-rock]] and back, with lavish prizes set out "midfield — blest tripods, fresh green crowns, and palms... armor, too, and robes infused with crimson dye; gold bars and silver" for the winners.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Mnestheus** ([[entities/mnestheus]]) — captain of the *Seabeast*; goal: avoid the shame of finishing last; resources: a crew of Hector's former companions, rallied by a speech mid-race
- **Gyas** ([[entities/gyas]]) — captain of the huge triple-banked *Chimaera*; goal: win; resources: the largest ship and most oarsmen, squandered by his own temper
- **Sergestus** ([[entities/sergestus]]) — captain of the *Centaur*; goal: win; resources: aggressive close-line sailing that backfires
- **Cloanthus** ([[entities/cloanthus]]) — captain of the *Scylla*; goal: win; resources: a well-drilled crew and, decisively, a vow of sacrifice to the sea gods
- **Aeneas** ([[entities/aeneas]]) — race's sponsor and judge; sets the course marker and all prizes

## What Happened

Lots are drawn for starting positions; the four crews — garlanded, bare-chested, and oiled — surge off at the trumpet. Gyas takes the early lead, with Cloanthus close behind and the *Seabeast* and *Centaur* contesting third. At the turning rock, Gyas orders his cautious helmsman Menoetes to hug the dangerous shoreline; when Menoetes swings wide to avoid a hidden ledge instead, Cloanthus slips inside him at the turn and takes the lead. Enraged, Gyas throws Menoetes overboard and takes the helm himself — to the crowd's laughter as the soaked old man climbs onto a rock, coughing up seawater.

Sergestus and Mnestheus, seeing Gyas slowed, fight for the newly open position; Sergestus, cutting the turn too aggressively, wrecks the *Centaur* on an offshore reef, shattering oars. Mnestheus's *Seabeast* pulls clear and closes on the leader, Cloanthus, in the final stretch. With the margin razor-thin, Cloanthus stretches his hands seaward and vows a sacrifice — a white bull, wine poured into the sea — to Neptune's court of sea deities if they grant him victory. Portunus, the harbor god, personally drives the *Scylla* home first.

## Outcome

Cloanthus is crowned winner, given a gold-embroidered cloak, three bullocks per ship-captain, wine, and a silver bar. Mnestheus takes second and receives a captured mail shirt — a trophy originally stripped by Aeneas from the slain Demoleos at Troy. Sergestus, despite wrecking, still limps the *Centaur* home under sail and is rewarded with bronze cauldrons, silver cups, and a slave woman skilled in weaving, "glad for the rescued ship and crew." Gyas's own final placement and reward are not recorded by the poem once he takes the tiller himself. ^[ambiguous]

## Open Questions

- The poem does not state whether Gyas's assault on his own helmsman carried any penalty or protest, unlike the foul in the following foot race — an odd asymmetry in how Virgil treats on-field misconduct between the two contests. ^[ambiguous]
