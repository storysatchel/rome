# Lex Licinia de Modo Agrorum (367 BC)

The *Lex Licinia de modo agrorum* is the first Roman *lex de modo agrorum* ("law on the amount of land") ancient sources know of — part of the Licinian-Sextian Rogations carried by the tribunes C. Licinius Stolo and L. Sextius Lateranus in 367 BC, traditionally capping an individual's landholding at 500 *iugera* (125 hectares) plus limits of 100 large and 500 small grazing animals. Surviving ancient testimony is fragmentary, contradictory, and late; virtually every aspect of the traditional account—its date, provisions, and whether it applied to public land, private land, or both—remains subject to competing reconstructions. ^[ambiguous]
- Whether the grazing limit (100 large and 500 small beasts) was part of the original 367 BC statute or represents a later addition remains unresolved, as sustaining herds of that size would have exceeded the pasture capacity of the early Roman territory.
