# The Assassination of Massiva and the Trial of Bomilcar

While King [[entities/jugurtha|Jugurtha]] of [[entities/numidia|Numidia]] was in [[entities/rome|Rome]] in 112 BC under a Senate-granted safe conduct, the consul Spurius Albinus — eager to manufacture a legitimate pretext for renewed war against Jugurtha — secretly urged a rival Numidian royal, Massiva (a grandson of Masinissa living as a refugee in the city), to petition the Senate for the Numidian crown. Jugurtha, learning of the scheme, ordered his trusted agent Bomilcar to have Massiva killed before the petition could gain traction.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Jugurtha** ([[entities/jugurtha]]) — goal: eliminate a rival claimant to his throne; resources: gold, a hired band of spies and killers, diplomatic immunity as a guest of the Senate
- **Massiva** ([[entities/massiva]]) — goal: claim the Numidian throne with Roman backing; stakes: his life
- **Bomilcar** ([[entities/bomilcar]]) — goal: carry out his king's orders without exposing him; resources: bribed informants who tracked Massiva's daily movements
- **Spurius Albinus (consul)** — goal: manufacture grounds to resume the war against Jugurtha by sponsoring a rival claimant

## What Happened

Bomilcar's hired spies learned Massiva's daily routine — his comings and goings, the places he visited — and set an ambush at a suitable hour. The plan went wrong in its execution: the hired assassin who struck the fatal blow was clumsy, and was seized on the spot before he could flee. Under pressure, the captured killer named Bomilcar as his employer. Because too many prominent Romans had an interest in seeing justice done, the case could not be buried as an ordinary street crime. Albinus and the Senate pressed for Bomilcar to be formally charged, even though his diplomatic status as part of Jugurtha's retinue technically placed him outside Roman jurisdiction. Bomilcar was bound over for trial, and Jugurtha initially supplied fifty sureties — acquaintances who stood bail for his appearance — apparently willing to let the trial proceed to preserve appearances.

## Outcome

Jugurtha reconsidered: no outcome of Bomilcar's trial could restore his own reputation in Rome, so rather than risk his agent revealing more, he smuggled Bomilcar out of the city and back to Numidia — forfeiting the bail of his fifty sureties. Within days the Senate ordered Jugurtha himself to leave Italy. As he departed, he is said to have looked back at Rome and remarked, "A city for sale and ripe for ruin, if only a purchaser can be found!" The episode destroyed what remained of Jugurtha's credibility among the Roman nobility and helped precipitate the renewed war that followed.

## Open Questions

- Greenidge does not identify the assassin by name or clarify exactly how the plot was first traced back to Bomilcar.
