# Sempronia

## Historical Identity and Lineage
Sempronia (*fl.* 1st century BC) is one of the most vividly characterized aristocratic women of the late Roman Republic, immortalized in Sallust's *Bellum Catilinae* (ch. 25). Modern prosopographical research by Olga Liubimova has definitively established her high-aristocratic pedigree:
- **Parentage:** Sempronia was the daughter of the famous popular reformer Gaius Sempronius Gracchus (tribune 123–122 BC) and his wife Licinia, daughter of P. Licinius Crassus Dives Mucianus (pontifex maximus and consul 131 BC). She was thus the direct niece of Tiberius Gracchus and the granddaughter of Cornelia, mother of the Gracchi.
- **Marriage and Descendants:** She married Decimus Junius Brutus (consul 77 BC). Through this union, she was the biological mother of Decimus Junius Brutus Albinus, the Caesarian admiral and assassin on the Ides of March 44 BC.

## Sallust's Famous Portrait
In *Bellum Catilinae* 25, Sallust interrupts his narrative of Catiline's conspiracy to provide an extraordinary literary monograph of Sempronia, portraying her as an archetype of aristocratic female agency, intellectual brilliance, and moral dissolution:
- **Intellectual Attainments:** Sallust records that Sempronia was "well-versed in Greek and Latin literature, could play the lyre and dance more skillfully than is necessary for an honest woman, and possessed many other accomplishments which minister to luxury."
- **Agency and Decisive Wit:** "Her wit was abundant; she could write verses, crack jokes, and converse in styles modest, tender, or wanton; in short, she possessed a high degree of charm and cleverness."
- **Crimes and Audacity:** Sallust accuses her of reckless audacity, having "often betrayed her word, repudiated debts by false oaths, been privy to murders, and plunged headlong into ruin through lust and luxury."

## Role in the Catilinarian Conspiracy (63 BC)
Sempronia was an active conspirator in the movement led by L. Sergius Catilina:
- Her house was utilized by P. Cornelius Lentulus Sura and the urban conspirators as a clandestine meeting ground.
- In Sallust (*Cat.* 40), when Lentulus seeks to recruit the ambassadors of the Allobroges into an armed rebellion against Rome, he sends Umbrenus to conduct the delicate treasonous negotiations specifically at Sempronia's house, because her husband Decimus Brutus was absent from Rome and she was known to be deeply devoted to Catiline's cause.
- Her radical political alignment reflects the enduring popular legacy of her father Gaius Gracchus, finding common cause with Catiline's debt-cancellation (*novae tabulae*) and anti-senatorial program.
