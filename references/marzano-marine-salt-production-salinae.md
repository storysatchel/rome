# Marine Salt Production in the Roman World: The Salinae and Their Ownership

## Coverage Note
Pioneering archaeological and economic synthesis by Annalisa Marzano (*Quaternary Science Reviews* 336, 2024, 108776; 8 pp.) assembling Mediterranean-wide archaeological and epigraphic evidence for coastal solar evaporation salt works (*salinae*) in the Roman world.

## Sampled Claims and Economic Revisionism
- **Refutation of the Universal State Monopoly Model:** Directly disproves the prevailing scholarly orthodoxy that Roman salt production was an uninterrupted state monopoly from the regal period onward. Marzano proves that during the late Republic and the first two centuries of the Principate, private ownership of coastal marine salt works was the norm, not the exception.
- **Distinction between Inland Mines and Coastal Pans:** Shows that while inland rock-salt mines (*salifodinae*) and the archaic pans at the Tiber mouth (*salinae Ostienses*) were public state property (*ager publicus* / state domain leased to publicani), coastal shorelines were classified by Roman jurists as *res communes omnium* (common to all), allowing private landowners to construct and operate solar evaporation pans without imperial concessions.
- **Archaeological Evidence from Salt Pans:** Examines key excavated coastal salinae, including the exceptionally well-preserved complex at O Areal near Vigo (Galicia, Spain) with stone evaporating basins, wooden channels, and crystallization ponds, and the large-scale coastal installations at Caunos in Caria (Turkey).
- **The Garum and Fish-Salting Nexus:** Demonstrates that the immense diffusion of private fish-salting workshops (*cetariae*) producing *garum* and *salsamenta* across Baetica, Lusitania, and North Africa required massive, competitive, and unencumbered commercial salt procurement, which a rigid state monopoly would have choked.
- **Late Imperial Monopolization:** Traces the actual emergence of coercive state monopolies and price-fixing to the late 3rd and 4th centuries AD under the fiscal distress of the Dominate.
