# Engraved Gems and Propaganda in the Roman Republic and under Augustus

## Coverage Note
Comprehensive archaeological and art-historical monograph by Paweł Gołyźniak (Archaeopress Roman Archaeology 65, 2020; 619 pp.) providing the first comprehensive analysis of engraved gemstones—intaglios used as personal seal rings and state cameos—as instruments of elite political communication, personal branding, and dynastic propaganda in Republican and Augustan Rome.

## Sampled Claims and Iconographic Corpus
- **Evolution of Republican Seal Devices:** Traces the transformation of gem engraving from Etruscan and Hellenistic mythological motifs into an explicitly Roman aristocratic idiom of ancestral achievement. Roman nobles selected signet motifs that broadcast family history: Scipio Africanus's portrait, Sulla's signet depicting Bocchus surrendering Jugurtha, Pompey's seal of three military trophies and a lion holding a sword, and Julius Caesar's armed Venus Genetrix.
- **Systematization of Glyptic Imagery under Augustus:** Demonstrates how Octavian/Augustus revolutionized glyptic media. Beginning with a sphinx (abandoned due to accusations of oriental monarchy), shifting to Alexander the Great, and ultimately commissioning his official court gem-cutter Dioskourides to engrave his idealized personal portrait, Augustus standardized imperial portraiture across signet rings throughout the Mediterranean administration.
- **State Cameos as Imperial Ideology:** Analyzes monumental multi-layered sardonyx cameos (e.g. the *Gemma Augustea* in Vienna and the *Grand Camée de France* in Paris) as masterworks of court political theology, displaying the deified Augustus enthroned beside the goddess Roma, flanked by personifications of the Oikoumene, Oceanus, and Tellus, with Tiberius stepping down from a triumphal chariot.
