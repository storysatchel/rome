# “Nubit amicus: Same-sex weddings in Imperial Rome”

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- Ancient evidence for same-sex weddings is scarce and often ambiguous between literal ceremony, metaphor, or mockery (pp. 89–91). ^[ambiguous]
- The 342 CE constitutio of Constantius and Constans in Codex Theodosianus appears to prohibit male-male weddings, though its wording is text-critically disputed (p. 90). ^[ambiguous]
- Historiographical accounts of Elagabalus and Nero involve gendered/emperor-specific scandal and cannot straightforwardly establish ordinary marriage (pp. 91–94). ^[ambiguous]
- Juvenal Satire 2 and… ^[ambiguous]
- Juvenal Satire 2 and Martial Epigrams 1.24 and 12.42 present ceremonies with conventional wedding forms (dowry, veil, torches, formulae), supporting the existence of private same-sex weddings among consenting men (pp. 95–97). ^[ambiguous]
- Roman law did not recognize these unions as *matrimonium*; ceremony and legal marriage were separable, while participants could treat the union seriously in private (pp. 98–100). ^[ambiguous]
