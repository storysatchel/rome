# Quintus Fabius Maximus Aemilianus

Biological son of L. Aemilius Paullus (by his first wife, Papiria), given up for adoption into the [[entities/clan-fabius|Fabii Maximi]] — probably by a Fabius Maximus who had been praetor in 181 BC — once that branch's male line had nearly died out in the 180s BC. See [[concepts/roman-elite-adoption-practices]] for the mechanics of the rescue adoption.

## Biography / Role

- Despite his adoption, he kept close ties to his biological father: he accompanied Paullus on the Macedonian campaign against King Perseus (171–168 BC), serving alongside Scipio Nasica on an operational feint of 5,000–8,000 troops that helped strategically encircle Perseus's forces ahead of the decisive battle at Pydna (168 BC).
- He and his younger biological brother, Scipio Aemilianus, both rode in Paullus's three-day triumphal procession after Pydna.
- Won the consulship for 145 BC and commanded in Spain against the Lusitanian leader Viriathus; after an initial legate's defeat near Urso, he deliberately avoided pitched battle with green recruits, building their discipline through minor skirmishes before engaging — a cautious approach McCall notes echoes Fabius Cunctator's strategy, though he flags the detail as possibly a fabricated annalistic borrowing rather than a verified fact. ^[ambiguous]
- Before campaigning, he made a special sacrifice to Hercules at Gades, which McCall reads as the Fabii Maximi "demonstrating religious piety and preserving their family status and prestige by cementing claims to descent from Hercules." ^[inferred]
- His command was extended as proconsul for 144 BC, possibly through the intervention of his biological brother Scipio Aemilianus, who reportedly spoke in the Senate against both candidate consuls for the Spanish command that year.
- He went on to rout Viriathus's forces and capture two cities before returning to Rome; later, as legate under Scipio Aemilianus during the final siege of Numantia (134–133 BC), he commanded one of the two long-term Roman siege camps.
- On Paullus's death, Scipio Aemilianus — heir to the far wealthier Scipio family fortune — ceded his own share of their biological father's estate to Fabius, who was comparatively less wealthy as a Fabius Maximus. Father of [[entities/fabius-maximus-allobrogicus]].

## Open Questions

- McCall cannot confirm whether the "cautious, disciplined-recruit-building" story from his Spanish campaign is a genuine record or a later annalistic echo of Cunctator's reputation projected onto a descendant. ^[ambiguous]
