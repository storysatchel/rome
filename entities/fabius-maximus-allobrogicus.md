# Quintus Fabius Maximus Allobrogicus

Biological son of [[entities/fabius-maximus-aemilianus]] (and thus a grandson of L. Aemilius Paullus), born into the [[entities/clan-fabius|Fabii Maximi]] rather than adopted. McCall ranks him "the third most famous of the Fabii Maximi," after Rullianus and Cunctator, and treats his career as the family's last major political and military success.

## Biography / Role

- His early career is obscure; a likely quaestorship (134 BC) came with decisive help from his biological uncle, Scipio Aemilianus, who came to the Campus Martius to campaign for his nephew's election and reportedly left having unexpectedly won a second consulship for himself instead.
- Probably served under his father and uncle during the Numantine War before becoming praetor and governor of Spain (123 BC), where he was publicly reprimanded by the tribune Gaius Gracchus for extracting grain from the provincials, which Gracchus had returned.
- As consul in 121 BC, campaigned against the Gallic Allobroges and the Averni, reportedly killing 120,000 Averni warriors — a figure McCall flags as an implausible ancient casualty inflation given "the proclivity of ancient sources to fabricate astonishing numbers of casualties." ^[ambiguous]
- Accepted the personal submission of the defeated Averni king Bituitus, who was brought to Rome and kept at Alba (the Senate judged it unsafe to return him to Gaul), while the Allobroges themselves formally surrendered.
- For the victory he earned the cognomen "Allobrogicus," built a battlefield trophy and two temples — one to Mars (the war-god) and one to Hercules, continuing the Fabii's centuries-long claim of descent from Hercules.
- Constructed the *fornix Fabianus*, a triumphal arch at the eastern end of the Forum Romanum — possibly the first triumphal arch in Roman history — decorated with statues not only of Fabius Maximus ancestors but also of Aemilius Paullus and Scipio Africanus, physically memorializing the family's adoptive lineage alongside its own. ^[inferred]
- McCall calls him "the last Fabius Maximus to win the consulship through a legitimate election, and the last to vanquish a significant foreign foe and claim great glory for himself and his family line" — marking the practical end of the family's run of independent political-military achievement.
- His son was later denied inheritance of Allobrogicus's estate by a praetor's 91 BC ruling, on the suspicion (per Valerius Maximus) that he would squander it immorally. His grandson, as curule aedile in 57 BC, restored the fornix Fabianus.

## Open Questions

- McCall's sources dispute whether a later Q. Fabius Sanga (senator in 63 BC, patron of the Allobroges) or the Caesarian suffect consul of 45 BC was Allobrogicus's direct descendant and arch-restoring grandson, or whether these were two different men; certainty "eludes" on this point.
