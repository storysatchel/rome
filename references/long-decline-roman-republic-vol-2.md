# The Decline of the Roman Republic, Vol. 2

## Coverage Note
Volume 2 of George Long's rigorous historical narrative (*The Decline of the Roman Republic*, London: Bell and Daldy, 1866; 513 pp.), covering the eventful decade from 107 to 86 BC in exhaustive political, legal, and military detail.

## Sampled Claims and Institutional Narrative
- **The Cimbric Crisis and Marian Army Reorganization:** Follows the catastrophic defeat of Caepio and Mallius Maximus at Arausio (105 BC) and Marius's emergency consulships. Details Marius's tactical reforms: replacing the manipular census militia with volunteer recruitment of the *capite censi*, adopting the silver eagle (*aquila*) as the sole legionary standard, and re-engineering the pilum with a wooden breakable pin to prevent enemy reuse.
- **The Second Sicilian Slave War (104–100 BC):** Analyzes the widespread insurrection in Sicily under Salvius (Tryphon) and Athenion, demonstrating how the island's brutal latifundia system endangered Rome's food security and required successive consular armies under Aquillius to suppress.
- **The Outbreak of the Social War (*Bellum Italicum*):** Reconstructs the escalating constitutional crisis over Italian allied enfranchisement: the conservative purge under the *Lex Licinia Mucia* (95 BC), the assassination of reforming tribune Marcus Livius Drusus (91 BC), and the armed secession of the Marsi, Samnites, and Picentes.
- **Enfranchisement Statutes and Sulla's March on Rome:** Examines the concessions granted under the *Lex Julia* (90 BC) and *Lex Plautia Papiria* (89 BC) admitting loyal and surrendering allies to Roman citizenship. Follows the subsequent factional clash over enrolling the new citizens across the 35 tribes, culminating in Sulpicius's alliance with Marius, Sulla's march on Rome with his legions, and the Marian-Cinnan terror of 87–86 BC.
