# Big Brother Is Watching You: Agentes in Rebus

## Coverage Note
Chapter 13 of Rose Mary Sheldon's monograph (*Intelligence Activities in Ancient Rome*, Routledge, 2005, pp. 266–287) analyzing the establishment, bureaucratic integration, and operational methods of the late Roman intelligence corps known as the *agentes in rebus*.

## Sampled Claims and Late Imperial Bureaucracy
- **Civilian Bureaucratic Intelligence:** Analyzes Diocletian's disbandment of the military frumentarii and the creation of the *agentes in rebus* as a civilian imperial corps (*schola*) subordinated directly to the *Magister Officiorum* (Master of Offices).
- **Hierarchical Rank and Career Progression:** Details the five internal promotional ranks (*equites*, *circitores*, *biarchi*, *centenarii*, *ducenarii*). After completing service in the central imperial palace, senior agentes were dispatched to the provinces as postal inspectors (*curiosi*) or appointed as executive heads of provincial bureaus (*principes officii*), effectively spying on provincial governors.
- **Surveillance and Religious Enforcement:** Demonstrates their key roles: inspecting the imperial post (*cursus publicus*), monitoring potential military usurpers, investigating treason (*crimen maiestatis*), and enforcing imperial orthodoxy by tracking down pagan worshippers and Christian heretics.
- **Venality and Delation:** Examines contemporary critiques by Ammianus Marcellinus and Libanius depicting the agentes in rebus as predatory informants (*delatores*) who fabricated false treason accusations against wealthy provincials to seize their estates.
