# Roman Lives: A Selection of Eight Roman Lives

## Coverage Note
Authoritative modern translation by Robin Waterfield, accompanied by extensive scholarly introduction, historical commentary, and source analyses by Philip A. Stadter (Oxford World's Classics, Oxford University Press, 1999; 538 pp.), presenting eight essential biographies from Plutarch's *Parallel Lives*: Cato the Elder, Aemilius Paullus, Tiberius and Gaius Gracchus, Marius, Sulla, Pompey, Caesar, and Antony.

## Sampled Claims and Biographical Methodology
- **Plutarch's Ethical-Political Biographical Program:** Stadter's introduction demonstrates that Plutarch (*c.* AD 46–120) composed comparative biographies (*bioi*), not chronological narrative histories (*historia*). His objective was moral and pedagogical: examining how individual statesmen navigated virtue (*arete*), political ambition (*philotimia*), and personal character under the corrupting pressures of imperial power.
- **Narrative Arc of Republican Dissolution:** The selected biographies form a coherent historical drama tracing the fall of the Republic: from the austere civic virtue of Cato the Elder and the disciplined triumph of Aemilius Paullus, through the populist fractures of the Gracchi, the brutal warlordism of Marius and Sulla, to the dynastic titanism of Pompey, Caesar, and Antony.
- **Preservation of Lost Republican Primary Sources:** The edition's explanatory notes document Plutarch's invaluable preservation of lost Republican accounts, including the memoirs of Sulla, the speeches of Gaius Gracchus, the histories of Posidonius, Gaius Fannius, and Asinius Pollio, and Augustus's private memoirs.
