## Sampled claims

- Religious specialists should be studied beyond formal priesthood, including entrepreneurs, healers, prophets, philosophers, clergy, and small-group organizers (editorial overview, EPUB lines 548–560, 612–624). ^[ambiguous]
- Religious experts attracted followers, packaged knowledge, and competed over religious authority (overview, lines 548–556). ^[ambiguous]
- Public priests could innovate while performing traditional duties; priesthood, emperor, choice, and power interacted in imperial Rome (chapter on public priests, lines 666–688). ^[ambiguous]
- Religious authority was contested across civic, Jewish, Christian, medical, and mystery-religion settings (contents and overview, lines 185–281, 580–640). ^[ambiguous]
- Bodies, clothing, gender, sexuality and bodily alteration served as signs in struggles over religious status (overview, lines 560, 628–640). ^[ambiguous]
- The “lived ancient religion” framework directs attention to practices and interactions rather than fixed polis-cult institutions alone (acknowledgements/overview, lines 299–307, 612–624). ^[ambiguous]
