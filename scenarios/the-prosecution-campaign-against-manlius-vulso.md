# Tribunes Nerva and Turdus's Campaign to Recall and Prosecute A. Manlius Vulso

In 178 BC the consul A. Manlius Vulso was assigned [[entities/gaul|Gaul]] as his province and launched a campaign against the Histri, a people on the northern Adriatic. His initial attack ended in defeat, and his colleague M. Iunius Brutus had to march to his aid before the two consular armies together broke Histrian resistance. Manlius's setback, however, provoked a political reaction at [[entities/rome|Rome]] the following year, where two tribunes sought to use legislative and judicial machinery to punish him.

## Parties & Stakes

- **A. Manlius Vulso** ([[entities/aulus-manlius-vulso]]) — consul of 178 BC, later holding a prorogued command against the Histri; goal: retain his command and avoid prosecution for the setback; resources: his continuing (prorogued) imperium and consular rank.
- **A. Licinius Nerva and C. Papirius Turdus, tribunes of 177 BC** ([[entities/licinius-nerva-and-papirius-turdus]]) — goal: strip Manlius of his prorogued command and clear the way to prosecute him once he lost magisterial immunity; resources: tribunician right to promulgate bills and address public *contiones*.
- **An unnamed colleague tribune** — goal: protect Manlius from the bill; resources: the tribunician veto (*intercessio*).
- **M. Iunius Brutus, consul of 177 BC** ([[entities/marcus-iunius-brutus-cos177]]) — goal: preside fairly over the year's business, including the elections, while his own colleague-successor situation was being contested; resources: consular authority to preside over the comitia.

## What Happened

Nerva and Turdus launched a public campaign in their *contiones* against Manlius, culminating in a bill designed to repeal his already-prorogued command in [[entities/histria|Histria]] — the necessary first step toward prosecuting him once he lost the immunity that came with holding imperium. ^[extracted] One of their own tribunician colleagues, however, vetoed the measure, blocking it from reaching a vote. ^[extracted] The consul Iunius Brutus, meanwhile, arrived in Rome to preside over the elections while this dispute over Manlius played out in the background. ^[extracted]

## Outcome

The veto preserved Manlius's command and shielded him from the threatened prosecution, at least for the time being — illustrating how a single tribune's intercession could neutralize a campaign mounted by two of his colleagues even when backed by public agitation over a genuine military failure.

## Open Questions

- Whether Manlius was ever brought to trial after his command finally ended is not stated in either source. ^[ambiguous]
- The vetoing tribune's identity and motive are not recorded. ^[ambiguous]
