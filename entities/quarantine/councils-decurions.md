# councils/decurions

Roman decurions occupied a privileged position within civic hierarchy. Their status was expressed not only in administration but also in distributions of money and food, where larger shares publicly marked their precedence over other groups.

## Ranking the civic community

One inscription discussed by Hemelrijk records a distribution on the dedication of a statue: decurions and their sons received eight sesterces each, Augustales and their sons six, the male populace four, and women two. The amounts made distinctions of civic standing visible. Prominent female city patrons could receive exceptional honours, but their prominence did not remove the subordinate place assigned to women generally in distributions.

## Municipal institutions and public cult

The possible elevation of Nijmegen to municipal status under Trajan offers a more uncertain connection between councils and civic religion. The city was renamed Ulpia Noviomagus, but the precise legal change remains disputed. Excavation at Elst dates the rebuilding of the exceptionally large sanctuary of Hercules Magusanus to around AD 100.

Van Rossum proposes that a new municipium might have redefined the obligations of its decurions toward public cults, with the rebuilt sanctuary reflecting the community’s new dignity. This links archaeological and institutional developments plausibly, but does not prove either the municipal grant or the proposed change in councillors’ duties.
