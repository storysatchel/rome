# Janus

Janus (*Ianus*) was a Roman deity associated with doors, gates and beginnings, and represented with a double head. His two faces and ritual precedence have been interpreted as signs of an abstract power of opening; that reconstruction should be distinguished from the ancient practices themselves. Identification of Janus as a sun or year god is contested.

## Shrines and public commemoration

Janus had more than one sacred site. C. Duilius vowed a temple during the naval battle of Mylae in 260 BC; its location near the harbor and the temple of Portunus has been interpreted in relation to Janus as a god of transitional spaces. This harbor temple should not automatically be identified with the shrine whose doors figured in declarations of war and peace.

The closing of the shrine of Janus in 29 BC followed the civil wars and accompanied the *augurium salutis*. One interpretation treats the closing as an antiquarian revival and proposes that its attribution to king Numa was invented around the earlier closure of 235 BC; that origin is a scholarly hypothesis, not a secure account of the ritual's beginning.

Augustus' *Res Gestae* presents the closing as marking peace obtained by victories throughout the Roman people's dominion on land and sea. The claim joins peace to military success and imperial power rather than demonstrating peace beyond Rome's dominion. There were three closures under Augustus; the date of the third is uncertain, since the proposed 2 BC date depends on the late writer Orosius and his Christian chronological argument.
