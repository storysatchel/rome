# Catiline's Trial for Extortion in Africa

Lucius Sergius Catilina served as praetor in 68 BC and the following year governed Africa, where — according to Pennell — "by his extortions he had obtained enough money, as he hoped, to purchase his election to the consulship." On returning to Rome he was impeached for misgovernment of the province, a charge that threatened to end his political ambitions before his famous conspiracy ever began.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Lucius Sergius Catilina** ([[entities/lucius-sergius-catilina]]) — goal: avoid conviction and preserve his path to the consulship; resources: extorted provincial wealth, Cicero's defense
- **Marcus Tullius Cicero** ([[entities/cicero]]) — goal: secure Catiline's acquittal; resources: oratory and "careful selection of a jury"
- **Unnamed African provincials** — stakes: the extorted wealth and justice for the governor's misconduct

## What Happened

Catiline was formally impeached for his conduct as governor of Africa. Cicero undertook his defense, and — notably — the jury was carefully selected to favor acquittal.

## Outcome

Catiline was acquitted, clearing the way for him to continue pursuing the consulship. His subsequent electoral failures, however, would push him toward the armed conspiracy of 63 BC that made his name notorious.

## Open Questions

- Pennell does not specify why Cicero defended Catiline at this stage, years before their later enmity during the conspiracy.
