# The Roman Carcer and Its Adjuncts

## Coverage Note
Definitive topographical and institutional investigation by T. J. Cadoux (*Greece & Rome* 55.2, 2008, pp. 202–221) establishing the physical layout, legal functions, and historical operations of the Roman state detention and execution facilities: the *Carcer*, the subterranean *Tullianum*, the auxiliary *Lautumiae* tufa quarries, and the *Scalae Gemoniae* (Gemonian Steps).

## Sampled Claims and Topographical Mechanics
- **Architectural Bipartition of the Prison:** Cadoux establishes the crucial distinction between the upper chamber (*Carcer* proper, traditionally attributed to King Ancus Marcius), utilized for custodial detention of accused individuals awaiting trial or judgment, and the lower subterranean chamber (*Tullianum*, attributed to Servius Tullius), a circular room roofed by a corbelled tufa vault entered only through a circular aperture in the floor.
- **Execution Machinery of the Tullianum:** Demonstrates that Roman penal law did not recognize imprisonment as a punitive sentence; the Tullianum functioned strictly as a place of summary execution for state enemies condemned for treason (*perduellio* / *maiestas*). Condemned captives were lowered through the hole into the darkness and strangled with a noose by the executioner (*carnifex*). Notable victims include Pleminius (204 BC), Gaius Gracchus's followers (121 BC), the Catilinarian conspirators Lentulus Sura and Cethegus (63 BC), and the Gallic warlord Vercingetorix (46 BC).
- **The Lautumiae Auxiliary Facility:** Reconstructs the ancient tufa quarries on the Clivus Argentarius behind the Curia as an expansive detention compound used when the tiny Carcer was inadequate, holding large numbers of military prisoners, hostages from rebellious allied cities, and rebellious slaves.
- **The Scalae Gemoniae and Punitive Humiliation:** Identifies the Gemonian Steps running down the Capitoline past the Carcer into the Forum as the designated site where the corpses of executed traitors were publicly displayed and exposed to popular mutilation before being dragged by iron hooks into the Tiber, denying the victim funeral rites and sepulture.
