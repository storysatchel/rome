# Constantine I

Constantine’s victory at the Milvian Bridge on 28 October 312 brought his rivalry with Maxentius to a decisive conclusion. It also ended the Praetorian Guard as an institution, linking a struggle for imperial power to a lasting change in Rome’s military landscape.

## From disputed succession to battle

After Constantius Chlorus died at York in 306, his army proclaimed his son Constantine emperor, outside the intended tetrarchic succession. Maxentius was proclaimed in the same year. The surviving praetorians at Rome supported Maxentius after reductions in their numbers and increased taxation under Galerius.

Constantine advanced south along the Via Flaminia. Maxentius had the numerical advantage, but his forces were driven toward the Tiber while the praetorians stood their ground. A pontoon bridge beside the Milvian Bridge collapsed under fleeing troops, and Maxentius drowned. The praetorians were left facing Constantine on the north bank. A relief later installed on Constantine’s arch commemorated drowning guardsmen in their distinctive scale armour.

## Dissolution of the Guard

Constantine did not replace the defeated praetorians with loyalists. He dissolved the Guard permanently and dispersed discharged men along the Rhine and Danube. The south and west walls of the Castra Praetoria were demolished; the north and east remained incorporated into the Aurelian Walls, with gates blocked. The dismantling of the camp made the institution’s abolition visible in the city itself.
