# Ti. Sempronius Gracchus (cos. 177, 163 BC)

Consul in 177 and 163 BC, censor, and augur — and father of the later reformist tribunes Tiberius and [[entities/gaius-gracchus]], a distinct and much earlier figure from either of his famous sons. While presiding over the 163 BC consular elections, the *primus rogator* (first voting unit) died mid-election, an unsettling incident the haruspices initially ruled had made him an unlawful presiding officer — a finding he rejected. Nearly a year later, while governing Sardinia, he spontaneously recalled a genuine ritual error he had committed and reported it to the augurs, forcing the sitting consuls for 162 BC to abdicate.
