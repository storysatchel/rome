# The History of the Progress and Termination of the Roman Republic, Vol. 6

## Coverage Note
Concluding volume of Adam Ferguson's major historical work (*The History of the Progress and Termination of the Roman Republic*, revised edition 1805/1813), examining the final transition from the battle of Actium (31 BC) through the constitutional foundation of the Augustan Principate (27 BC), the domestic administration of Augustus, and the reign of Tiberius down to the death of Tiberius and accession of Gaius Caligula (AD 37).

## Sampled Claims and Constitutional Analysis
- **The Nature of Augustan Autocracy:** Ferguson argues that Augustus created an absolute monarchy veiled under republican terminology. By disavowing royal titles and nominally 'restoring the republic' to the Senate and People in 27 BC, Augustus secured uncontested control through the permanent combination of proconsular command (*imperium maius*) over the frontier legions and civil supremacy via the tribunician power (*tribunicia potestas*).
- **Subversion of Republican Assemblies:** Demonstrates how the popular assemblies (*comitia*) were systematically hollowed out, their electoral functions reduced to rubber-stamping imperial candidates and their legislative role replaced by senatorial decrees (*senatus consulta*) and imperial edicts.
- **Tiberian Autocracy and Sejanus:** Analyzes the psychological and institutional hardening of imperial rule under Tiberius. Traces the terrifying ascendancy of Praetorian Prefect Lucius Aelius Sejanus, the proliferation of treason prosecutions (*maiestas*), the complete transfer of magistrate elections from the people to the Senate, and the total paralysis of senatorial independence.
