# The Lex Repetundarum and the Political Ideas of Gaius Gracchus

## Coverage Note
Fundamental epigraphic, legal, and constitutional study by A. N. Sherwin-White (*The Journal of Roman Studies* 72, 1982, pp. 18–31) investigating the epigraphic text of the Republican extortion law (*Lex Repetundarum*, preserved on the Naples bronze tablet / *Tabula Bembina*) and its role in the legislative ideology of Gaius Gracchus.

## Sampled Claims and Epigraphic Analysis
- **Epigraphic Identification and Dating:** Examines the surviving bronze inscription, dating it to 123/122 BC and attributing its statutory provisions to the Gracchan judiciary law (the *Lex Sempronia Iudiciaria* or *Lex Acilia*).
- **The Equestrian Jury Revolution:** Establishes that the central innovation of the statute was the absolute exclusion of senators and their immediate relatives (fathers, sons) from the jury list (*album iudicum*) of the standing extortion court (*quaestio perpetua de repetundis*), transferring judicial trial power exclusively to the equestrian order (*equites*).
- **Procedural Safeguards for Provincials:** Details the statute's revolutionary procedural protections for allied and provincial subjects:
  1. Compulsory appointment of competent Roman patrons to advocate for provincial plaintiffs.
  2. The award of double damages (*duplum*) assessed on convicted senatorial extortionists.
  3. The grant of Roman citizenship, immunity from military service (*militiae vacatio*), and exemption from civic taxes (*tributi vacatio*) to any non-citizen provincial prosecutor who secured the conviction of a corrupt Roman magistrate.
  4. Secret voting by ballot (*per tabellam*) dropped into a locked bronze urn to shield jurors from aristocratic intimidation.
- **Refutation of the Mommsenian Tyranny Thesis:** Directly refutes Theodor Mommsen's canonical theory that Gaius Gracchus introduced equestrian juries as an act of calculated malice to foment class warfare between the Senate and the Knights and establish a personal tyranny. Sherwin-White proves that Gracchus's procedural architecture aimed at genuine impartiality, constitutional equity (*aequitas*), and the deterrence of imperial misgovernment.
