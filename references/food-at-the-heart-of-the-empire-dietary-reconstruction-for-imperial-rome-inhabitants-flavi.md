- (p.1 abstract) Archaeological and biological evidence supports dietary reconstruction for people buried in Rome in the 1st–3rd c. CE. (p.1 abstract) Carbon/nitrogen isotope analysis covers 214 human bones from six funerary contexts, with 17 contemporary faunal baselines. (p.1 abstract; pp.10–12) Results indicate predominantly terrestrial diets based on C3 plants; C4 plants were consumed but less widespread. (p.1; pp.10–12) Environment/topographical location shaped food access, including among lower social classes. (p.2) Grain distribution became a state concern from the Gracchan period; the Principate’s *annona* was central to emperor–citizen relations. (p.3) Grain/cereals formed the caloric backbone, supplemented by vegetables, legumes, meat, cheese, wine and fish; garum complicates isotopic interpretation. (pp.10–12) No evidence supports massive marine-fish or exclusive C4 consumption, though occasional marine/freshwater resources cannot be excluded. ^[ambiguous]
