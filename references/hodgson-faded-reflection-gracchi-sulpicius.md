# 'A Faded Reflection of the Gracchi': Ethics, Eloquence and the Problem of Sulpicius in Cicero's De Oratore

## Coverage Note
Detailed rhetorical and political study by Louise Hodgson (*The Classical Quarterly* 67.1, 2017, pp. 163–181) analyzing Cicero's complex characterization of the orator Publius Sulpicius Rufus in *De Oratore* (dramatically set in 91 BC, published in 55 BC).

## Sampled Claims and Ethical-Political Dynamics
- **Sulpicius as Aristocratic Exemplar:** In 91 BC, Sulpicius was the favored pupil of Lucius Licinius Crassus, celebrated for his elevated, grand, and tragic rhetorical style (*grandis et tragicus*), operating firmly within the consensus of the senatorial optimates.
- **The Tragic Reversal of 88 BC:** Reconstructs Sulpicius's radical political shift during his 88 BC tribunate: breaking with the Senate, allying with Gaius Marius, proposing the redistribution of new Italian citizens across all 35 tribes, and transferring the lucrative Mithridatic command from consul Sulla to Marius. This triggered Sulla's historic first march on Rome and Sulpicius's immediate proscription and decapitation.
- **Eloquence Divorced from Moderation:** Argues that Cicero frames Sulpicius as a tragic warning. Sulpicius represents the inherent danger when immense rhetorical power lacks philosophical moderation (*temperantia*) and civic equilibrium, degenerating into a "faded reflection" of Gaius Gracchus that precipitates civil war.
