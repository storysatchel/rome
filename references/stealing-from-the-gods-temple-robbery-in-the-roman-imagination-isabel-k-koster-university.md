# *Stealing from the Gods: Temple Robbery in the Roman Imagination*

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- Roman discourse distinguishes acceptable state-directed removal of sacred wealth from unacceptable temple robbery; temple robbery is framed as individual moral failure rather than collective Roman policy; narratives construct sacredness as context-dependent and unstable; the *sacrilegus* is an outsider who rejects Roman religious/property norms; accusations appear especially in warfare, imperial expansion, crisis, and character assassination; recurring examples include Cicero/Verres, Livy, Caesar, Appian, Pausanias, and emperors. ^[ambiguous]
