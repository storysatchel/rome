# Fabius Gurges Fines a Bloc of Married Women for Adultery

Early in his public career, likely while serving as a junior magistrate such as aedile, Quintus Fabius Maximus Gurges — son of the great general Rullianus and already saddled with a nickname mocking his spendthrift habits — is recorded by Livy as having fined a group of married Roman women collectively for committing adultery.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Quintus Fabius Maximus Gurges** ([[entities/quintus-fabius-maximus-gurges]]) — goal: build a public reputation for moral rigor early in his magisterial career, perhaps to offset his personal reputation for profligacy; resources: magisterial authority to prosecute and levy fines.
- **The accused matrons** (unnamed in the surviving record) — goal: unknown; outcome: collectively found guilty and fined.

## What Happened

Livy's brief notice records only that Gurges, in a junior magisterial role, fined a bloc of married women for adultery — a case McCall notes echoes his father Rullianus's own moral-crusading debut prosecuting poisoners as a young aedile, suggesting the family cultivated a pattern of launching political careers through high-profile moral prosecutions. ^[inferred] No individual names, specific charges, or trial procedure survive beyond this bare notice. ^[extracted]

## Outcome

The matrons were collectively found guilty and fined; no further details of amounts or consequences survive.

## Open Questions

- The precise magistracy Gurges held at the time is not recorded; McCall assumes aedile but flags this as uncertain. ^[ambiguous]
- The number and identities of the women involved, and the specific circumstances of the prosecution, are not preserved.
