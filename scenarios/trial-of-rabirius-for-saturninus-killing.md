# The Perduellio Trial of Gaius Rabirius, 63 BC

Thirty-seven years after the lynching of the tribune Saturninus and the praetor Glaucia in 100 BC, the tribune Titus Labienus — Saturninus's own nephew — revived the archaic and almost-forgotten procedure of *perduellio* (an ancient form of treason trial, judged by two specially appointed officers, the *duumviri*) to prosecute the elderly senator Gaius Rabirius for having personally killed Saturninus during the chaos.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Titus Labienus** ([[entities/titus-labienus]]) — goal: avenge his uncle's death and, more immediately, strike a symbolic blow against the senate's authority to use the "final decree" (*senatus consultum ultimum*) to justify killing citizens without full trial; resources: tribunician office, political backing including Caesar's cooperation as duumvir.
- **Gaius Rabirius** ([[entities/gaius-rabirius]]) — goal: avoid a capital conviction for actions taken decades earlier under the senate's emergency authority; resources: Cicero as his defense advocate, the senate's institutional stake in defending the legitimacy of the final decree.
- **Julius Caesar** ([[entities/julius-caesar]]) — goal: use his role as one of the two duumviri presiding over the archaic procedure to undermine senatorial authority and burnish his own popularis credentials; resources: the revived procedural machinery itself.

## What Happened

Labienus structured the prosecution to put the entire 100 BC crisis — and by extension the legitimacy of the senate's "final decree" used to suppress Saturninus — on trial alongside Rabirius personally. Cicero, defending Rabirius, delivered a speech walking the jury (the populus itself, sitting as a quasi-jury in this ancient form of trial) through a detailed thirty-seven-year-old sequence of events, assuming they still possessed detailed civic knowledge of a crisis from before many of them were born. The trial produced visible crowd reactions: when Cicero expressed his wish that Rabirius actually had killed Saturninus (implying it would have been glorious to do so), the statement provoked an audible shout from the audience. Labienus, for his part, theatrically displayed Saturninus's ancestral funeral mask (*imago*) during the proceedings — deliberately echoing the emotional effect Caesar himself had achieved six years earlier when he displayed the long-suppressed imago of Marius at his aunt's funeral.

## Outcome

The archaic procedure never ran to full capital execution; the case was ultimately halted on a technicality when the presiding praetor took down the flag flying over the [[entities/janiculum|Janiculum hill]], an ancient signal that stopped proceedings before the populus could vote. The affair nonetheless achieved its larger symbolic purpose: it put the senate's authority to kill citizens under an emergency decree on public trial, three decades before the question would become acute again in the Catilinarian crisis of that very same year.

## Open Questions

- Whether Caesar's role as duumvir was by chance appointment or a deliberately engineered position is debated by modern scholars.
