# The Ambitus Conviction That Annulled the Consular Election of Sulla and Autronius

In the consular elections for 65 BC, P. Cornelius Sulla and P. Autronius Paetus were the victorious candidates. Before they could take office, however, both were convicted of electoral bribery (*ambitus*) under the governing lex Calpurnia. The conviction did not merely cost them the consulship — it triggered expulsion from the senate itself, and the affair generated a follow-up legislative fight the next year over softening the penalty.

## Parties & Stakes

- **P. Cornelius Sulla** ([[entities/publius-cornelius-sulla-consul-designate]]) — consul-elect for 65 BC; goal: hold the consulship he had won; resources: electoral victory, aristocratic connections (kinsman of the dictator Sulla).
- **P. Autronius Paetus** ([[entities/publius-autronius-paetus]]) — co-consul-elect; goal: same as Sulla; resources: electoral victory, senatorial rank.
- **Prosecutors under the lex Calpurnia** (unidentified here) — goal: enforce the anti-bribery law and bar the pair from office; resources: the statute itself and evidence of bribery.
- **L. Caecilius Rufus, tribune of 64 BC** ([[entities/lucius-caecilius-rufus-tribune64]]) — goal: ease the penalty imposed on the convicted pair by proposing a law canceling part of the lex Calpurnia's sanction; resources: tribunician right of legislative initiative (*promulgatio*).

## What Happened

Sulla and Autronius won the consular election for 65 BC but were subsequently convicted of ambitus — systematic bribery of the electorate — under the lex Calpurnia, the statute then governing the offense. Conviction carried a double penalty: disqualification from the consulship they had just won, and expulsion from the senate altogether. ^[extracted] The following December, soon after taking office, the tribune L. Caecilius Rufus proposed a bill that would have canceled part of the penalty the lex Calpurnia prescribed for the crime — an apparent attempt to soften the consequences retroactively, presumably on behalf of Sulla and/or Autronius. ^[inferred] The outcome of Caecilius's bill remains uncertain. ^[ambiguous]

## Outcome

Sulla and Autronius lost the consulship and their senate seats; the vacancy had to be filled afresh. The disgraced pair's resentment is traditionally linked by later sources to their alleged involvement in conspiratorial activity around Catiline in the following years, though this connection remains an interpretation rather than an established consequence of the conviction. ^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- Whether Caecilius Rufus's relief bill ever passed remains uncertain. ^[ambiguous]
