# cursus publicus

The *cursus publicus* was the Roman state transport organization, carrying official passengers and state goods as well as facilitating messages. Its intensive use of roads helped shape accommodation along them, particularly *mansiones* and *mutationes*.

## Augustan origins

The system developed from measures introduced by Augustus to improve communication. It was initially known as the *vehiculatio*; the name *cursus publicus* is first attested in the fourth century AD. The exact date of its foundation is unknown. Suetonius describes an initial arrangement using young relay runners, followed by couriers travelling in wagons who could carry a message throughout the journey and answer questions about it on arrival.

## Transport rather than a corps of postmen

The organization used pedestrians, riders, and wagons, with rapid travel becoming more important as the empire expanded. It had no couriers of its own. Couriers could sometimes use roadside lodging, but ordinarily did not change animals; urgent messages could authorize fresh mounts under a relay arrangement.

The system was administered by a *praefectus vehiculorum*. Holders are known in the first century AD, although the office’s precise origin is uncertain. The extensive late antique legal evidence, especially the Theodosian Code, reflects both growing administrative complexity and the burdens and abuses the transport system imposed on local populations.
