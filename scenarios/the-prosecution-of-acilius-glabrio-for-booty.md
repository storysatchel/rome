# The Tribunician Prosecution of Acilius Glabrio Over War Booty (189 BC)

In early 189 BC, M'. Acilius Glabrio — consul of 191 BC and victor over Antiochus III at [[entities/thermopylae|Thermopylae]] — was accused by two tribunes of giving an inadequate account of the booty taken in his campaign. Cato, who had served as a subordinate officer under Glabrio in Greece, appeared as a prominent witness against his own former commander, delivering the speech *In M'. Acilium Glabrionem*.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Manius Acilius Glabrio** ([[entities/manius-acilius-glabrio]]) — goal: defend his record and reputation ahead of the censorial elections of 189 BC, in which he and Cato were rival candidates; resources: consular prestige, his Thermopylae victory.
- **Cato the Elder** ([[entities/cato-the-elder]]) — goal: discredit a rival censorial candidate and burnish his own reputation for probity; resources: firsthand testimony as a former subordinate, oratorical skill.
- **The two accusing tribunes** (unnamed in the surviving sources) — goal: formally charge Glabrio with inadequate accounting of booty; resources: tribunician power to initiate the prosecution.

## What Happened

The surviving fragment of Cato's speech shows him taking the occasion to emphasize his own excellent handling of military matters during the [[entities/hispania|Spanish]] campaign, implicitly contrasting it with Glabrio's alleged deficiencies in accounting for the Thermopylae booty. ^[extracted] The prosecution came at a politically charged moment: Cato and Glabrio were about to stand as rival candidates for the censorship of 189 BC. Rivalry for that office may have motivated or intensified the attack, though this cannot be proved. ^[inferred]

## Outcome

The precise verdict is not recorded in the surviving sources, but Glabrio's candidacy for the 189 BC censorship failed — Cato himself also lost that election, to L. Valerius Flaccus's benefit three years later in 184 BC.

## Open Questions

- The two tribunes who brought the charge are not named in the surviving record.
- Whether the prosecution was motivated primarily by genuine financial malfeasance or by censorial electoral rivalry remains disputed. ^[ambiguous]
