# Marcus Claudius Marcellus

Marcellus's sack of Syracuse in 211 BC became a landmark in Rome's acquisition of Greek art as war spoils. It need not mark an unprecedented break: captured artworks had long served as religious and political trophies.

## Biography / Role

- The acquisition and display of captured art predated Marcellus. Religious precedents included Camillus's *evocatio* of Juno from Veii in 396 BC.
- Cult statues from defeated cities — Veii's Juno, Praeneste's Jupiter Imperator, Volsinii's Vortumnus, and Falerii's Janus and Minerva Capta — could be ritually "absorbed" into Roman state religion as tokens of divine favor. On this interpretation, the Syracusan spoils extended an existing religious-political logic rather than creating a wholly new practice. ^[inferred]
- Greek artists, including the painter Metrodorus and sculptors Timarchides, Polycles, Dionysius, Philiscus, and Pasiteles, came to Rome across several generations of conquest. Their influx intensified especially after L. Scipio Asiagenus's victory over Antiochus III in 190 BC and L. Aemilius Paullus's conquest of Macedon in 168 BC. Marcellus's spoils were thus an early, prominent instance within a wider pattern, not its sole driver.

## Open Questions

- How far did the Syracuse spoils alter the scale and reception of an older practice of acquiring sacred and artistic trophies?
