# Harriet Fertik

Harriet Fertik is a classical scholar whose work examines the political, legal, and cultural dimensions of the domestic sphere in imperial Rome.

## The Domus as a Political Space

The Roman *domus* was never a secluded private retreat in the modern sense: for the senatorial elite and the imperial family, domestic architecture operated as an active stage for public patronage, social dominance, and political visibility. Under the Principate, one-man rule made the emperor's residence the primary center of state decisions, rendering the ruler's domestic life subject to intense public scrutiny and aristocratic surveillance.

Roman jurisprudence reflected fluid boundaries between public and domestic domains, distinguishing *res publicae* (belonging to the citizen body), *res privatae* (individual property), and *res communes omnium* (shared natural resources). Domestic architecture accommodated these tensions: reception halls, *atria*, and dining spaces served as semi-public venues where access, bodily demeanor, and aristocratic hospitality reinforced imperial hierarchy.
