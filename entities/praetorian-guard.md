# Praetorian Guard

The Praetorian Guard was the imperial bodyguard organized on a permanent basis under Augustus. It had Republican antecedents in the temporary personal guards of Roman generals. Its history combined military service with influence at the center of imperial politics, ending when Constantine I disbanded it in 312.

## Cohorts rather than a single Roman title

“Praetorian Guard” is a modern collective name. Romans called the bodyguard the *cohortes praetoriae*, or praetorian cohorts. Their headquarters likewise had several related names, including *Castra Praetoria* and *Castra Praetorianorum*, rather than a single title corresponding exactly to the modern phrase.

The cohorts were exceptionally well paid, prestigious, and influential within the Roman military. Their appearances in ancient sources are intermittent, however, limiting attempts to reconstruct every aspect of their organization and experience.

Cohort numbers, unit sizes, pay, and assigned duties varied with precedent and circumstances. Recruitment could occur at the beginning of a soldier's career or through later promotion from a legion; membership continued to confer standing in retirement.

## Political survival and final disbandment

The crisis of 193 exposed both the Guard's leverage and its limits. The praetorians supported Didius Julianus on the promise of a large payment, but provincial armies, not the bodyguard, decided the ensuing civil war. Septimius Severus cashiered the existing Guard and reconstituted it with trusted legionaries from his own forces. Institutional survival therefore did not mean continuity of personnel. The crisis can be interpreted as a consequence of the bodyguard's privileged proximity to power rather than an isolated lapse by individual soldiers.

The final dissolution came in a different political conflict. After the praetorians supported Maxentius against Constantine, Constantine disbanded them in 312 and dispersed individual soldiers to frontier garrisons. This ended about 340 years of permanent institutional existence, in addition to the earlier ad hoc Republican role.
