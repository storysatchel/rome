# Cicero's Defense of Sextus Roscius on a False Parricide Charge

In 80 BC, in the aftermath of Sulla's proscriptions, Sextus Roscius of [[entities/ameria|Ameria]] was accused of murdering his own father. Cicero’s defense alleged that the charge was false and that associates of Sulla’s freedman Chrysogonus wanted the father’s estate declared forfeit as proscribed property so it could be bought up cheaply; the surviving defense allegation is not independent proof of their motives.^[inferred] This was the first major public oration of the twenty-six-year-old Marcus Tullius Cicero, who had to tread carefully given that the prosecution was effectively backed by men close to the dictator Sulla.

## Parties & Stakes

- **Sextus Roscius** ([[entities/sextus-roscius]]) — goal: avoid conviction and execution for parricide; stakes: his life and his father's estate
- **Marcus Tullius Cicero** ([[entities/cicero]]) — goal: launch his legal career with a daring win; resources: oratory, careful handling of Sulla's circle
- **Associates of Chrysogonus** — alleged by the defense to be seeking Roscius’s inherited property under cover of the proscriptions; unnamed in the summary account used here

## What Happened

Roscius was accused of murdering his father. Cicero presented the prosecution as a cover for listing the dead man’s estate among the proscribed and selling it cheaply to insiders connected to Sulla’s regime.^[inferred] Cicero took on the defense despite the political risk of implicitly criticizing men in Sulla's orbit, and argued the case successfully before a Roman jury.

## Outcome

Cicero won the case; Roscius was acquitted. The victory "guaranteed for him a brilliant future" as an orator and marked the true beginning of Cicero's public career. Cicero reportedly left [[entities/rome|Rome]] for a period of study in [[entities/achaea-greece|Greece]] and [[entities/asia-province|Asia]] shortly afterward, which later writers connected to prudence about having crossed interests close to Sulla.^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- The specific accusers and the evidence presented are not detailed in the brief account used here; the property-seizure explanation should be distinguished from an independently established finding of fact.

## Sources

It does not name the accusers or detail the evidence.
