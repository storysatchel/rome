## Concepts

- [[concepts/ager-publicus|Ager Publicus]] — Roman state-owned public land, seized from defeated enemies; legally distinct from private land but increasingly held as de facto private possessio by occupants until privatized away by 59 BC. ( #ager-publicus #public-land #roman-law)
- [[concepts/art-and-political-propaganda|Art and Political Propaganda]] — Romans used captured art, sculpture, coinage, and engraved gems as interlocking media for elite and state self-presentation and family promotion. ( #propaganda #material-culture #elite-self-presentation)
- [[concepts/caesars-dictatorship-institutional-program|Caesar's Dictatorship: The Institutional Program]] — Marin's chapter-level account of the specific governing measures Caesar enacted as dictator — magistracy counts, senate size, provincial terms, debt relief, calendar — beyond the bare fact of "dictatorship." ( #julius-caesar #dictatorship #roman-magistracies)
- [[concepts/catos-investment-farm-model|Cato's Investment Farm Model]] — Astin's close economic reading of De Agricultura shows Cato's 100-240 iugera cash-crop estates were run on mixed financial and social-prestige logic, not pure profit-maximization. ( #cato-the-elder #de-agricultura #investment-farming)
- [[concepts/clientelization-mechanics-in-the-roman-bazaar|Clientelization Mechanics in the Roman Bazaar]] — The concrete mechanisms behind Bang's "clientelization" — fides as tradeable reputation, cult as a trust-signaling infrastructure, and diaspora merchant networks producing durable, exclusionary trade relationships. ( #clientelization #fides #trust)
- [[concepts/roman-collegia-courts-and-dispute-resolution|Collegia, Courts, and Commercial Dispute Resolution in the Bazaar]] — How Roman trade associations (collegia), patronage, and informal arbitration substituted for weak state legal enforcement in resolving commercial disputes. ( #collegia #roman-law #arbitration)
- [[concepts/consulship|Consulship]] — The Republic's supreme annual magistracy, combining absolute military command abroad with civil headship at Rome — presiding over the senate, assemblies, and law. ( #consulship #imperium #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/contio|Contio]] — The non-voting public meeting where Republican magistrates addressed the Roman People, serving as the central (and contested) site of elite-mass political communication. ( #contio #roman-oratory #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/elite-family-strategy|Elite Family Strategy]] — The practices — continuous political achievement, inherited pedigree claims, and strategic adoption — by which Roman aristocratic families sustained multi-generational standing. ( #elite-families #roman-aristocracy #adoption)
- [[concepts/establishment-of-overseas-provincial-governorships|Establishment of Overseas Provincial Governorships]] — How Rome's informal mid-Republican hegemony hardened into standing praetorian governorships in Sicily, Sardinia, and Spain, and the extortion scandals that forced the first permanent accountability court in 149 BC. ( #provincial-governor #praetor #repetundae)
- [[concepts/fall-of-the-roman-republic|Fall of the Roman Republic]] — Three accounts — Mommsen, Marin, Watts — converge on a gradual, multi-generational collapse from the Gracchi to Augustus, but disagree on whether Caesarian monarchy was tragedy or cure. ( #fall-of-the-republic #political-violence #julius-caesar)
- [[concepts/formula-togatorum|Formula Togatorum]] — How Rome's Italian allies were obligated for troops — treaty basis, proportional quota formula, and why manpower-only extraction was the structural alternative to citizen tributum. ( #formula-togatorum #socii #allied-manpower)
- [[concepts/hellenism-and-roman-elite-identity|Hellenism and Roman Elite Identity]] — Roman elite engagement with Greek culture in the 3rd-2nd c. BC was a coherent, confident process of self-definition, not an anxious love-hate contradiction. ( #hellenism #roman-identity #cato-the-elder)
- [[concepts/honor-economy-and-private-armies|Honor Economy and the Rise of Private Armies]] — Watts traces the mid-Republic's "honor economy," in which the state alone issued political reward, as the social glue later dissolved when politicians began fielding armed, personally loyal followings. ( #honor-economy #private-armies #political-violence)
- [[concepts/land-and-agrarian-conflict|Land and Agrarian Conflict]] — Rome's recurring struggle over public-land distribution, from early agrarian laws to the Gracchi — though sources disagree on whether elite land-grabbing or population growth was the real driver. ( #agrarian-reform #land-conflict #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/laudatio-funebris|Laudatio Funebris (Roman Funeral Eulogy)]] — The Roman aristocratic funeral eulogy genre, which ancient authors themselves accused of fabricating triumphs, offices, and patrician status — with named, traceable instances of the distortion. ( #laudatio-funebris #roman-historiography #source-criticism)
- [[concepts/legal-categories-of-ager-publicus|Legal Categories of Ager Publicus]] — Rome's public land was not one legal category but at least five — occupatorius, quaestorius, in trientabulis, censorius, scripturarius — each created at a different date for a different fiscal or political purpose. ( #ager-publicus #roman-law #land-tenure)
- [[concepts/optimates-and-populares|Optimates and Populares]] — Rather than organized parties, *optimates* and *populares* names a contested rhetorical vocabulary and a durable, largely unchallenged ideology of senatorial hegemony performed at the *contio*. ( #optimates #populares #roman-political-ideology)
- [[concepts/popular-assemblies-rome|Popular Assemblies of Rome]] — Rome's voting bodies — curiate, centuriate, and tribal *comitia* plus the *concilium plebis* — elected magistrates and passed law, distinct in function and jurisdiction from the deliberative *contio*. ( #popular-assemblies #comitia #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/precedent-escalation-in-marin|Precedent-Escalation as a Mechanism in Marin's Narrative]] — Marin's running device of labeling successive acts "constitutionally unprecedented" tracks a specific chain — 132 BC exile law, Marius's repeat consulships, Sulla's march on Rome, proscription, dictatorship length — each explicitly cited as license for the next. ( #precedent #constitutional-crisis #marin)
- [[concepts/provinces-of-the-roman-empire|Provinces of the Roman Empire]] — How provincia shifted from a task to a territory; a gazetteer of named provinces; provincial urbanism as an exportable Roman city-model; and the empire's eventual collapse in the West. - [[concepts/quaestorship-and-provincial-finance|Quaestorship and Provincial Finance]] — How the quaestorship grew from a consul's field paymaster into the Republic's standing financial-administrative link to provincial governors, tracked through office numbers and named cases. ( #quaestor #cursus-honorum #provincial-finance)
- [[concepts/roman-commercial-credit-and-partnership|Roman Commercial Credit and Household Partnership]] — Roman trading capital was organized through household-based instruments — societas partnerships, the peculium, and bottomry loans — not independent firms or banks. ( #credit #societas #peculium)
- [[concepts/roman-domestic-life|Roman Domestic Life]] — An umbrella view of Roman daily routine and family life, from the agrarian rhythm of the day to the wider economy of the household. ( #domestic-life #daily-routine #household-economy)
- [[concepts/roman-economy-and-trade|Roman Economy and Trade]] — Rome's economy ran on personalized, high-uncertainty 'bazaar' exchange and tribute extraction rather than an integrated market; concrete trade-route geography maps Bang's comparative model. ( #roman-economy #trade #bazaar-model)
- [[concepts/roman-elite-adoption-practices|Roman Elite Adoption Practices]] — Roman aristocratic adoption transferred a son between patrician gentes to preserve a family's name and property, not to formalize sentiment — illustrated by the Fabii Maximi's near-extinction rescue. ( #adoption #elite-families #roman-aristocracy)
- [[concepts/roman-house|Roman House]] — The physical Roman house and villa — its layout and rural buildings — as both an architectural artifact and an organizing space for household and medical life. ( #domestic-architecture #pompeii #domestic-medicine)
- [[concepts/roman-household|Roman Household]] — The Roman household (familia) as a social and legal unit of free, freed, and enslaved members bound together under the authority of the pater/mater familias. ( #familia #patria-potestas #household-economy)
- [[concepts/roman-household-staff|Roman Household Staff]] — Cato's and Varro's prescriptions for staffing a working Roman farm — the overseer (vilicus), housekeeper (vilica), and laborers — and their defined duties and rations. ( #vilicus #vilica #slavery)
- [[concepts/roman-identity-and-cultural-memory|Roman Identity and Cultural Memory]] — How Romans constructed a distinctive collective identity through founding myth, selective engagement with Hellenism, family exemplars, and material propaganda. ( #roman-identity #cultural-memory #founding-myth)

- [[concepts/roman-magistracies|Roman Magistracies]] — The Republic's expanding set of annual offices — consuls, praetors, aediles, tribunes, quaestors, censors — each with distinct powers, census-based eligibility, and assembly affiliations. ( #magistracies #cursus-honorum #roman-republic)
- [[concepts/roman-military-organization-republic|Roman Military Organization (Republic)]] — The structure, recruitment, and record-keeping of the Republican army, from clan-based early warbands through the census-based manipular legion to the eventual cohort-based late-Republic army. ( #roman-army #manipular-legion #dilectus)
- [[concepts/roman-oratory-and-political-persuasion|Roman Oratory and Political Persuasion]] — Oratory was one component — alongside ancestry, wealth, patronage, military achievement, charisma — of a Roman politician's "public profile," central at the *contio* but never a strict prerequisite. ( #roman-oratory #rhetoric #political-career)
- [[concepts/roman-philosophy-schools|Roman Philosophy Schools]] — Epicureanism and Stoicism offered Romans opposing accounts of cosmic order and the grounds of a fearless, virtuous life. ( #epicureanism #stoicism #roman-philosophy)
- [[concepts/roman-population-growth-debate|Roman Population Growth Debate (High Count vs Low Count)]] — A live quantitative dispute over 2nd-century-BC Roman demography — Lo Cascio's 'high count' (explosive growth, millions of hidden slaves) versus Brunt/Hopkins's 'low count' and De Ligt's intermediate revision. ( #demography #population-growth #census)
- [[concepts/roman-provincial-administration|Roman Provincial Administration]] — Rome's gradual, improvised system for governing and extracting resources from conquered territory — allied manpower levies, war finance via tributum, and a slowly centralizing quaestorial bureaucracy. #### Province (13)

- [[entities/asia-province|Asia (Roman Province)]] — Rome's wealthy western-Anatolian province, inherited from Pergamon's bequest in 133 BC, staging ground for the Mithridatic Wars and home to Mytilene. ( #location-province #asia #roman-province)
- [[entities/britannia|Britannia]] — Roman province of Britain, governed in the 190s AD by Clodius Albinus, whose legions he led into Gaul to challenge Septimius Severus. ( #location-province #britannia #britain)
- [[entities/cyrenaica|Cyrenaica]] — Roman province on the North African coast west of Egypt, home to the city of Cyrene and the philosopher-bishop Synesius. ( #location-province #cyrenaica #libya)
- [[entities/egypt|Egypt (Ptolemaic/Roman)]] — The Nile kingdom of Egypt, Ptolemaic in the 2nd century BC and later a Roman province; seat of Alexandria, gateway to Red Sea and Indian Ocean trade. ( #location-province #egypt #ptolemaic-kingdom)
- [[entities/gaul|Gaul (Gallia)]] — The broad Celtic-inhabited territory north and west of Italy, encompassing Roman Cisalpine Gaul (northern Italy), Transalpine/Narbonensis Gaul, and the independent Gallic lands Caesar later conquered. ( #location-province #gaul #gallia)

- [[entities/india|India (Ancient)]] — Destination of Eudoxus of Cyzicus's pioneering monsoon-guided sea voyages from Ptolemaic Egypt, source of the spice cargoes seized by Egyptian customs. ( #location-province #india #foreign-trade)

#### Region (16)

- [[entities/liguria|Liguria]] — The coastal and mountainous region of northwestern Italy inhabited by the Ligurians, home to the allied city of Genua and the inland Langenses Viturii, whose 117 BC boundary dispute Rome arbitrated. ( #location-region #liguria #italy)
- [[entities/numidia|Numidia]] — An independent North African kingdom bordering Roman Africa, ruled by Jugurtha during the Jugurthine War (112-106 BC) that made Marius's career; not itself a Roman province during the Republic. ( #location-region #numidia #jugurtha)

- [[entities/samnium|Samnium]] — The mountainous region of south-central Italy inhabited by the Samnites, against whom Rome fought three major wars (343-290 BC) — frequent setting of the Fabii's early military and command disputes. ( #location-region #samnium #italy)
- [[entities/umbria|Umbria]] — The region of central Italy east of Etruria, home to Interamna and the long-running Velinus Lake drainage dispute with neighboring Sabine Reate. ( #location-region #umbria #italy)
- [[entities/venetia|Venetia]] — The region of northeastern Italy inhabited by the Veneti, home to Ateste and Vicetia, whose 134 BC boundary dispute Rome arbitrated. ( #location-region #venetia #italy)
