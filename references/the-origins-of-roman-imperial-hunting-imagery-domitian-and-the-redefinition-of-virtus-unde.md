# “The Origins of Roman Imperial Hunting Imagery: Domitian and the Redefinition of *Virtus* under the Principate”

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- sampled claims are preserved in the source-isolated candidate artifact; no full-book inference. ^[ambiguous]
- Later imperial hunting imagery is abundant, but no clear Republican or Augustan precedent establishes the tradition (`Introduction`, PDF p. 1 / printed p. 221). ^[ambiguous]
- A bronze equestrian statue from the Sacellum of the Augustales at Misenum is best restored as Domitian hunting a crouching lion, rather than fighting a barbarian (introduction and statue analysis, PDF pp. 1–8 / printed pp. 221–228). ^[ambiguous]
- This identification moves imperial hunting imagery back by at least roughly thirty years and places it within first-century attempts to redefine *virtus* (introduction, PDF p. 1 / printed p. 221). ^[ambiguous]
- Domitian adapted Greek/Philhellenic and Hellenistic hunting iconography into Roman concepts of *virtus*, with Pompeian domestic imagery as an important contemporary context (introduction, PDF pp. 1–3 / printed pp. 221–223). ^[ambiguous]
- Earlier imperial efforts by Gaius and Nero to redirect *virtus* met resistance, while Domitian’s model helped make hunting an acceptable imperial and elite display in subsequent centuries (introduction, PDF pp. 1–3 / printed pp. 221–223). ^[ambiguous]
