# “‘Quibus permissum est iura condere’: Some Ideas on the Origin of the *Ius Respondendi*”

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- no securely attested Augustan license called *ius respondendi* is required by Gaius 1.7; *quibus permissum est iura condere* means authority to explain/interpret law, not legislative power; juristic authority traditionally derived from reputation and court recognition; Justinian retroactively reframed juristic authority through the Institutes and Digest; the 426 Law of Citations, not an Augustan license, supplied later statutory authority for named jurists; Masurius Sabinus is the clearest explicit case of imperial authorization, under Tiberius. ^[ambiguous]
