# C. Verres

C. Verres was a Roman officeholder and governor of Sicily whose career became the target of Cicero’s Verrine speeches. Those speeches use accusations of temple robbery to connect his earlier service in the East with his provincial conduct, while reshaping the histories of the sites he visited.

## A geography of accusation

Verres’s career took him from Italy to the East, back to Rome, and finally to Sicily. Cicero encourages his audience to interpret offences committed along this route as acts of temple robbery. Places thus help connect otherwise separate episodes into a portrait of persistent misconduct.

Cicero also suppresses a site’s earlier history of plunder when that history would weaken Verres’s apparent singularity. By leaving previous robbers unnamed, he can present Verres as the first or most consequential violator, even where that portrayal is historically inaccurate. Connections with earlier villains depend partly on the audience’s own knowledge. The resulting picture is an argumentative construction, not a transparent record of every site’s history.

## Officeholding before Sicily

Verres served as quaestor to Gnaeus Papirius Carbo in Cisalpine Gaul before abandoning him in 84 BCE. Cicero’s account associates the departure with embezzlement. Four years later Verres joined Gnaeus Cornelius Dolabella’s staff in Cilicia, then became a prosecution witness when Dolabella faced an extortion trial on returning to Rome.

Köster shows how these earlier episodes prepare the portrait of the later governor. Cicero credits Verres with leadership, rather than mere participation, in the removal of gold from Athena’s sanctuary at Athens, even though the earlier trial had associated the crime with Dolabella. The speeches redistribute responsibility as well as connecting places. Their allegations therefore need attribution rather than repetition as an unmediated career record.
