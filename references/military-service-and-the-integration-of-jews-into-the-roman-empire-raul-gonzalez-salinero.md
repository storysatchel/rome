# *Military Service and the Integration of Jews into the Roman Empire*

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- Evidence from Josephus, legislation, inscriptions, papyri, Roman, Christian and rabbinic literature supports Jewish military service from the late Republic through Justinian (Introduction, pp. 1–2). ^[ambiguous]
- Evidence from Josephus, legislation, inscriptions, papyri, Roman, Christian and rabbinic literature supports Jewish military service from the late Republic through Justinian (Introduction, pp. 1–2).   - Jewish military participation was diverse and cannot be reduced to a single “normative Judaism”; practices varied across communities and periods (Introduction, pp. 2–4).   - Jewish exemptions from service in some periods coexisted with recruitment and service; historiography often overstated incompatibility between Judaism and military life (contents chs. 2–4; Introduction).   - Hellenistic precedents include Elephantine frontier garrisons and Jewish settlers/soldiers under Alexander and the Ptolemies, including mixed units and military colonies (ch. 1, pp. 8–15).   - Roman Jewish soldiers served in regular and exceptional contexts, while late Christian imperial policy increasingly restricted Jewish military participation (contents, pp. vii–viii). ^[ambiguous]
