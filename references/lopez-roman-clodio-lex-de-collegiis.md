# Publio Clodio y la lex de collegiis

## Coverage Note
Substantive socio-legal study by Luis Manuel López Román (*Espacio, Tiempo y Forma, Serie II, Historia Antigua* 22, 2009, pp. 117–126) investigating the historical nature, social cohesion, and political impact of the *Lex Clodia de collegiis* (*lex de collegiis restituendis novisque instituendis*) passed by tribune Publius Clodius Pulcher in 58 BC.

## Sampled Claims and Institutional Analysis
- **The Social Reality of Roman Collegia:** Rejects the traditional senatorial characterization of collegia as mere violent street gangs (*operae*). Demonstrates that urban associations were legitimate, deeply rooted mutual-aid institutions: trade guilds (*collegia fabrum*, *dendrophororum*, *pistorum*), burial societies (*collegia funeraticia*), and neighborhood shrines (*collegia compitalicia*) centered on the worship of the Lares Compitales.
- **The Senatus Consultum of 64 BC:** Details how the aristocratic Senate, terrified by the electoral mobilization and strike potential of organized plebeian guilds during the political agitation of the 60s BC, issued an emergency decree dissolving all collegia deemed contrary to public order.
- **Clodius's Re-institutionalization:** Explains the radical nature of the *Lex Clodia de collegiis* of 58 BC: it not only restored the ancient suppressed guilds but explicitly authorized the founding of entirely new associations (*novisque instituendis*). This enabled Clodius to systematically enroll shopkeepers (*tabernarii*), artisans, freedmen, and urban slaves into organized voting blocks and protective assemblies.
- **Popular Sovereignty and Political Agency:** Concludes that Clodius acted as a patron and mediator for genuine urban lower-class grievances, transforming collegia into an institutionalized counter-weight to aristocratic clientela in the public contiones and legislative assemblies.
