# An Imperial Image: The Bath Gorgon in Context

> [!tldr] The Gorgon from the Temple of Sulis Minerva at Roman Bath combines local sacred imagery with the imperial visual culture shared by Rome and the provinces.

## Scope and thesis

The pediment from the Temple of Sulis Minerva at Bath dates to the late Neronian or early Flavian period. The Gorgon, winged Victories, oak wreaths, and star imagery combine a local spring sanctuary with motifs associated with Roman victory and imperial power.

Comparisons with first- and second-century adaptations of Forum of Augustus imagery in Gaul and Spain suggest that provincial monuments participated in a visual language of empire while adapting it to local contexts. ^[inferred]

## Bath and the imperial network

- The sanctuary at Aquae Sulis connected local religious traditions to Roman monumentalization of the springs.
- Pedimental imagery shaped meaning through placement, movement, and audience experience rather than as isolated symbols.
- Comparisons across provincial bathing contexts show that imperial imagery could be locally reworked rather than copied mechanically.

This makes the article useful alongside [[entities/rome]] and the vault's work on [[concepts/roman-provincial-administration]].

## Open questions

- How did different audiences distinguish local divine identities from imperial visual claims in provincial sanctuaries? ^[ambiguous]
- Which features of the Bath pediment were selected for local reasons rather than inherited from metropolitan models? ^[ambiguous]
