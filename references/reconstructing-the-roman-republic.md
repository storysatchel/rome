## Sampled claims

- Modern scholarship’s debate concerns the political culture, institutions, procedures, hierarchy, participation, authority, and power of the *libera res publica* (preface and ch. 1, EPUB lines 316–322). | ^[ambiguous]
- The book surveys modern research on Roman Republican political culture and the conceptual categories used to describe it (PDF pp. 9–16). ^[ambiguous]
- Modern scholarship’s debate concerns the political culture, institutions, procedures, hierarchy, participation, authority, and power of the *libera res publica* (preface and ch. 1, EPUB lines 316–322). ^[ambiguous]
- One traditional model treats Republican politics as aristocratic factional competition among leading families (ch. 1). ^[ambiguous]
- A revisionist alternative emphasizes popular assemblies, voting, citizen sovereignty and the fluidity of the elite (ch. 1). ^[ambiguous]
- These orthodox and revisionist interpretations require sustained comparison rather than reduction to a single simplified account. ^[ambiguous]
- Prosopography is useful for careers, office-holding and social groups but is dangerous when treated as a complete explanation of political action (ch. 1, EPUB lines 386–390). ^[ambiguous]
- The book develops theoretical, methodological, and empirical approaches to Roman political culture across nine chapters (contents, EPUB lines 143–205). ^[ambiguous]
- Older scholarship emphasized aristocratic oligarchy and family factions as the effective structure of politics (PDF pp. 16–24). ^[ambiguous]
- Popular assemblies and participation provide an alternative focus to the older emphasis on aristocratic oligarchy and family factions. ^[ambiguous]
- Fluid associations complicate rigid factional schemas; this is a methodological caution rather than proof that aristocratic influence was absent. ^[ambiguous]
- Prosopography remains valuable but supplies only partial aid for questions such as popular acceptance of aristocratic government and Roman expansion (PDF pp. 23–27). ^[ambiguous]
- Nine thematic chapters move from constitution and social structure to concepts, political culture, consensus and symbolic capital (PDF contents pp. 6–8). ^[ambiguous]
