# Marcus Antonius (Mark Antony)

Mark Antony is the sixth and chronologically latest of van der Blom's non-Ciceronian case studies, his career extending into the triumviral period after 43 BC.

## Biography / Role

- Van der Blom's case-study chapter on Antony is organized around the theme of "career-making amid crisis," covering his triumviral period after 43 BC as the chronological endpoint of her study (which begins with Gaius Gracchus's tribunates in 123–122 BC).
- She classes Antony, alongside Pompey and Piso Caesoninus, as a "reactive" rather than active orator — speaking when magistracy required it but relying on other channels (visual spectacle, military reputation, patronage) to build his political profile rather than actively seeking out oratorical occasions. None of these three was designated a "good orator" by contemporaries or posterity.
- Van der Blom's closing argument — that a rewritten *Brutus* judged by the ability to project a coherent public narrative rather than oratorical skill — explicitly names Antony as one of the figures (alongside Piso, Cato, Sulla, Catiline, and Clodius) who would be newly admitted under this broadened criterion, having been excluded from Cicero's own narrower, oratory-focused history of orators.
