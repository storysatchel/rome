# *Roman Imperialism*

## Coverage note

This staged reference consolidates title-page, contents, introduction, and representative sampled evidence only; it does not claim full-book coverage. ^[ambiguous]

## Sampled claims

- Rome’s military and diplomatic expansion created a Mediterranean-wide empire over roughly 800 years (abstract, PDF p. 8). ^[ambiguous]
- Scholarship moved from defensive-versus-aggressive explanations toward theoretically informed analysis of systemic and discursive pressures (abstract and contents, pp. 6–8). ^[ambiguous]
- Roman imperialism research draws on ancient literary sources, modern theories, diplomacy, warfare, soft power, frontiers, race/ethnicity and Romanization (contents pp. 6–8). ^[ambiguous]
- Defensive imperialism (Frank and predecessors) explains expansion through fear, accidents, security and diplomatic constraints, while Harris emphasizes warrior-aristocratic ideology and conquest (PDF pp. 9–14). ^[ambiguous]
- Informal control and hegemony matter alongside formal annexation; Doyle defines empire as metropolitan control over a subordinate periphery’s domestic and foreign policy (PDF pp. 24–25). ^[ambiguous]
- International-relations realism explains interstate behavior through anarchy, security competition and worst-case assumptions, but is criticized as ahistorical and insufficiently attentive to honor, prestige and morality (PDF pp. 15–17). ^[ambiguous]
